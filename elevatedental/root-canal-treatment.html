Stage 01, the anatomy

What is inside a tooth

Inside every tooth is a chamber, and running down each root from it, a set of very narrow canals.

They hold the pulp, which is nerve tissue and blood vessels. When decay, a crack or an injury lets bacteria reach that pulp it becomes inflamed, then it dies, and the infection travels down the canal and out through the end of the root into the bone around it. That is what produces the throbbing, the swelling, and the tenderness when you bite.

The treatment removes that tissue. Each canal is cleaned along its whole length, shaped so it can be filled, irrigated repeatedly with disinfecting solution, then sealed with gutta percha and a sealer. The access opening is closed afterwards.

What it solves is a plain choice, keep the tooth or lose it. Once the pulp is beyond recovery there is no third option in which the tooth is left alone and gets better.

The honest part

Reaching the canals means taking structure out of the middle of the tooth, and a hollowed out back tooth splits under chewing force. That is why a crown is part of this treatment on a molar rather than an optional extra afterwards, and why the quote you are given should state both items. A treated tooth that splits usually cannot be saved.

Stage 02

Whether your tooth can be kept

Treatment is indicated when the pulp is irreversibly inflamed or has already died.

Lingering pain to hot or cold after the stimulus is gone Pain on biting A tooth that has darkened A swelling, or a small bump on the gum near the root An area of infection showing on a radiograph below the tooth

It is also carried out on a badly fractured tooth, and as a planned step before some crown and bridge work where the pulp is already compromised. If one of the three exits below describes your tooth instead, we would rather say so at the assessment than after you have paid for a treatment that was never going to hold.

Exit 01 Split root

A vertical root fracture

A root split down its length cannot be saved, and cleaning the inside of it changes nothing about the split. The tooth comes out and the conversation moves to what replaces it.

Oral surgery
Exit 02 Nothing to build on

Too little left above the gum

A clean canal is no use if there is not enough tooth standing above the gum to build a restoration onto. Replacement is planned instead, with an implant, a bridge or a removable appliance.

Dental implants Dental bridge
Exit 03 Lost support

Advanced bone loss around it

A tooth loosened by advanced gum disease does not become stable because the inside of its root is clean. The problem is outside the root, and that is where the treatment has to go.

Periodontics
Re-entry Second attempt

Treated years ago, sore again

This is retreatment, and it is common. The tooth is reopened, the old root filling is taken out, the anatomy is reassessed and the whole canal system is cleaned and sealed properly a second time. Many teeth already written off are kept this way.

Stage 03, diagnosis to review

The eight clinical steps

Eight steps, in this order, with the minutes each occupies at the chair.

Most cases take one to three visits of sixty to ninety minutes. Molars carry more canals than front teeth and take longer. Curved canals, retreatment and a tooth with an active abscess take longer still, and the plan you are handed states the sequence for your tooth rather than for a typical one.

Diagnosis to review, step by step
StepWhat happensHow long
01 Consultation and diagnosis Examination, sensitivity and percussion testing, and radiographs. Imaging in three dimensions is taken where a flat film cannot answer the question. The written quote is handed over here. 45 to 60 minutes
02 Anaesthesia and isolation The tooth and the tissue around it are numbed. A rubber dam is then placed to isolate the tooth, which keeps saliva and its bacteria out of the canals and is the standard of care for this work, not an optional refinement. First 15 minutes
03 Access A small opening is made through the biting surface, or through the back of an incisor, to reach the chamber. 15 to 20 minutes
04 Cleaning and shaping Every canal is located, measured to its full working length, cleaned, shaped so it can be filled, and irrigated again and again with disinfecting solution. This is the step that determines the outcome. Everything before it is preparation and everything after it is closure. 30 to 60 minutes
05 Medication and temporary seal Where a second visit is planned, an antibacterial dressing is left in place and the tooth is sealed temporarily for one to two weeks. Where a second visit is planned
06 Filling The canals are filled with gutta percha and sealer, and a radiograph confirms the fill has reached the correct length before anything is closed over it. 30 to 45 minutes
07 Core build up and the definitive restoration The tooth is rebuilt, and a crown is fitted on back teeth and on any incisor that has lost substantial structure. One to two weeks later
08 Review A radiograph is taken to confirm that the bone around the root is healing. This appointment belongs to the work, not to an upsell attached after it. At six to twelve months

Stage 04, the bench and the imaging

Who does this, and what they can see

This is endodontics, the recognised specialty concerned with the inside of the tooth and the tissue around the root tip, practised here in house as a main clinical discipline.

If the case then needs a crown, or turns out to need removal and replacement instead, that side of the plan is handled by a specialist inside the same practice rather than by a referral out.

The instrument that changes the plan

3D CBCT imaging, available at all four clinics.

A conventional radiograph flattens a three dimensional root system into a single image. That is how a fourth canal in an upper molar, a second canal in a lower incisor, a curve, a resorption defect or a small lesion at the root tip gets missed. A scan shows the anatomy in three dimensions before any opening is made, which is the reason a difficult case does not have to be sent somewhere else.

We name the systems because a name can be checked. An adjective cannot.

Sol Dental Laser

Every peso amount on this page is representative and anchored to published market ranges rather than a fee Elevate has quoted. What you pay is quoted per treatment, after a clinical assessment.

What moves the final figure

  • Which tooth it is. An incisor has one canal. A premolar usually has one or two. A molar commonly has three or four, and every one of them is located, cleaned, shaped and filled separately. This is the largest single variable and you can count it yourself.
  • How difficult the anatomy is. A curve, a calcified canal, an extra canal, an open root end or an active abscess each add chair time.
  • Whether it is a second attempt. Reopening a tooth and clearing out what was placed the first time is more work than starting clean.
  • Imaging. A scan, where the case needs one, is quoted as its own line.
  • The restoration afterwards. The core build up and the crown are separate items, and on a back tooth the crown belongs to the plan. Your quote states both, so the full figure is visible at the start rather than at the end.

The two paths, without numbers on them

You can have the tooth taken out instead, and sometimes that is the right call. But a gap does not stay a gap: the teeth beside it tilt, the tooth above or below it drifts down, and the bite changes. Replacing an extracted tooth with an implant or a bridge generally costs more than keeping the one you have. Both paths are laid out with their costs at the consultation, and the choice is yours to make with the figures in front of you.

Stage 06, how it is judged

How anyone knows it worked

The day your pain stops is not the day anyone knows this succeeded.

What stops first is measurable and quick. Pain from an irreversibly inflamed pulp usually settles within a few days of the canals being cleaned, and the tenderness to biting fades as the inflammation at the root tip resolves.

What matters is read on an image. Where infection had eaten into the bone at the root tip, that bone fills back in once the canal system has been properly sealed, and the follow up radiograph is where anyone can see whether it has. That is why the review appointment is part of the treatment and not an extra, and it is the only honest way to tell you whether the tooth has healed.

Stage 07, the end of the route

Eight answers, none of them folded away

Every answer below is open where it sits. Nothing here is behind a control, a form or a telephone call.

Aftercare, the first week

  • Do not chew on that tooth until the definitive restoration is on it, particularly while a temporary filling is holding the access cavity closed.
  • Take painkillers as directed for the first few days rather than waiting to see whether you need them.
  • Brush and floss the tooth exactly as you would any other.
  • Telephone the clinic if swelling increases, if pain worsens after the third day, or if the temporary filling comes out.

If a tooth is painful right now, say so when you book, so the appointment is given enough chair time to treat it rather than only to look at it.

Does it hurt?

The tooth is numbed before any work begins and it is kept numb throughout. Most of the pain people associate with this treatment comes from the infection that brought them in, not from the work that resolves it. Expect the tooth to be tender to biting for two to four days afterwards, which is normal and responds to ordinary painkillers taken as directed.

How many visits will it take?

One to three, depending on the tooth and on whether there is an active infection. Front teeth are commonly completed in a single sitting. Molars and abscessed teeth are more often done across two.

Why do I need a crown afterwards?

Because a treated tooth is more brittle than a live one and has had structure taken out of its middle. On a back tooth the cap holds the remaining walls together against chewing force, and a tooth that splits usually cannot be rescued. The crown protects the work you have already paid for.

Can I just have it taken out instead?

You can, and occasionally that is the right call. Read the two paths at stage 05 before you decide, because the consequences of a gap are the part that is easy to underestimate on the day.

It was treated before and still hurts.

That is retreatment and it is common rather than exotic. The tooth is reopened, the previous filling material is removed, the anatomy is reassessed on CBCT, and the whole system is cleaned again. Many teeth that were written off are kept this way.

Will the tooth go dark?

A front tooth can discolour afterwards. It is treatable, usually by whitening the tooth from the inside, or with a veneer or a cap. Raise it at the consultation if the tooth shows when you smile, so it is planned for rather than dealt with as a surprise later.

Do I need antibiotics?

Not routinely. Antibiotics do not reach the inside of a dead canal, which is the whole reason cleaning it is the treatment. They are prescribed where there is spreading infection or systemic involvement, and not as a substitute for the work.

Which clinic can I book?

There are four clinics in Metro Manila: Mitsukoshi BGC, Shangri-La Plaza, Bonifacio Stopover BGC and Alabang Town Center. Which of them schedule this work, and on which days, is still being confirmed with the practice, so book through the appointment control and you will be placed at the branch that can take your case.
