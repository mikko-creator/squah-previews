Removable partial dentures, tooth gaps

Go to stage 01
01
Stage 01, three parts, three jobs

What the object is

It is a base carrying replacement teeth, held in place by the natural teeth you still have.

Retention, which is what stops it lifting, comes from clasps that engage those natural teeth. Support, which is what stops it sinking, comes from a combination of those same teeth and the ridge of gum and bone underneath. Every design decision on this appliance is a negotiation between those two demands.

The problem it answers is several gaps that a bridge cannot span and that implants are not going to fill, whether for anatomical, medical or timing reasons. Filling those spaces returns chewing to that side of the mouth, stops the teeth beside the gaps drifting into them, and gives the lip and cheek something to rest against again.

The honest part

Nothing is cut away to fit this. That is its great advantage over a bridge. What it does instead is lean on the teeth it clasps, so those teeth carry load and hold plaque that they did not before. The appliance protects the gaps and raises the stakes on what is left, and both halves of that sentence are true at once.

Stage 02, the three constructions

Three ways to build it

Three constructions are in common use, and each buys one thing by giving up another.

Your prosthodontist names the construction for your case and the reason for it at the assessment, before any impression is taken. If the reason offered to you is not a clinical one, ask for the clinical one.

Build 01

Acrylic

A gum coloured base with no metal framework. It is the quickest to make and the easiest to add to later, which is exactly why it is the usual choice as an interim appliance while something else is planned or heals. The plate has to be thicker to be strong, and thickness is what the tongue notices.

Fastest, and the usual interim
Build 02

Cast cobalt chrome

A cast metal framework under a smaller acrylic saddle. The rigidity lets the plate be thinner and lets the load sit on the teeth through machined rests rather than pressing into the gum. It is generally the more comfortable long term appliance, and it needs one extra appointment to try the framework in on its own.

Thinner, more rigid, one visit longer
Build 03

Flexible thermoplastic

A nylon base with tooth coloured or gum coloured retention instead of visible metal. It is chosen when a clasp would show in a position you cannot accept. The trade is rigidity: a flexible base transmits more of the load into the gum, and it cannot be adjusted or relined as readily as the other two.

No visible metal, less rigid

Stage 03

Whether it fits your mouth

This answers several missing teeth, in more than one part of the arch or in a span too long to bridge, in a mouth that still has sound natural teeth worth keeping.

It suits patients who have too little bone for implants and do not want grafting. It also works as an interim appliance while implants integrate, so you are not toothless during the healing months.

Turning 01 One gap only

Something fixed, not something you take out

If you are missing a single tooth between two healthy neighbours, you should not be wearing anything removable for it. A bridge or an implant is the better treatment, it stays in, and it does not clasp the teeth on either side.

Dental bridge Dental implants
Turning 02 Almost nothing left

A full set, or an implant retained overdenture

If almost no teeth remain in the arch there is not enough left to clasp onto, and an appliance that clasps nothing simply rocks. The honest alternatives are a complete set or an overdenture anchored on implants, and the choice between those two is its own conversation.

Complete dentures Prosthodontics
Turning 03 Loose abutments

Gum treatment, before any design is settled

If the remaining teeth are already loose from gum disease, clasping onto them accelerates their loss. Periodontal treatment comes first, the response is measured, and the design is decided afterwards against teeth whose prognosis is actually known.

Periodontics Scaling and root planing

04
Stage 04, the daily part

What it asks of you

This appliance asks something of you every day, and the asking does not stop.

It has to come out and be cleaned daily, over a basin of water so a dropped appliance lands in water rather than on porcelain, with a denture brush and soap or a denture cleaner rather than abrasive toothpaste. It soaks overnight in a denture solution or in plain cold water, never hot.

The teeth it grips then need more attention than they did before, not less, because a clasped tooth collects plaque along the line the metal sits on. And it is usually left out at night, which gives the gum and those teeth several hours to recover.

If that routine is not realistic for you, say so at the consultation. It is not a failure and it is not a judgement. It changes which appliance you should be offered, and it is far better said before the impressions than discovered a year later.

Four operations, every day

  • Out, and brushed over water.
  • Your own teeth cleaned separately, with attention to the surfaces the clasps rest on.
  • Soaked overnight, cold.
  • Back in, in the morning, and checked that it seats without force.

The routine above is the manufacturer neutral standard of care for a removable appliance. No claim is made here about how long yours will serve.

Stage 05, examination to adjustment

The clinical sequence

Eight appointments, in this order, with the minutes each occupies at the chair.

Most cases run to four or five visits across two to six weeks, spaced by the laboratory work between them. Cases with a cast framework take longer than acrylic ones, because the framework is tried in on its own before a single tooth is added to it.

Eight appointments, in order
VisitWhat happensHow long
01 Consultation and assessment Examination, radiographs, and a reading of what is left in the arch, the bone supporting it and the shape of the ridge. Design and material are agreed. The written quote is handed over here. 45 to 60 minutes
02 Preliminary treatment, where needed Fillings, extractions, crowns on the teeth that will carry clasps, or gum treatment. Each is quoted separately and finished before anything is started. A separate visit
03 Primary impressions A first record of each arch, from which a custom tray is built for the accurate record that follows. 30 minutes
04 Definitive impressions and design The custom tray records teeth and ridge accurately. For a cast case, rests and clasp positions are planned on the model, and small shaping adjustments are made to the natural teeth so the framework seats correctly. 45 minutes
05 Framework try in, cast cases only The metal is fitted on its own, with no teeth on it, to confirm it seats fully and passively. A framework that does not seat here is remade rather than persuaded. 30 minutes
06 Tooth try in The replacement teeth are set in wax so you can see and approve position, shade and, above all, where any clasp will show when you smile. Changes are free at this point and expensive after it. 30 to 45 minutes
07 Insertion The finished appliance is fitted, the bite is read, the clasps are adjusted, and you are taught to seat and remove it with your fingers rather than by biting it into place. 45 minutes
08 Review and adjustment Sore spots in the first fortnight are normal and are relieved by adjusting the fitting surface. These visits are part of the treatment, not evidence that something went wrong. One to two short visits over a month

The wax try in at visit 06 is where a case is actually judged.

Stage 06, how the number is built

What it costs

The anchor is ₱25,000, a representative market figure for this appliance in the Philippines and not a quotation from Elevate.

The real number is confirmed in writing after the clinical assessment. We do not price an appliance without examining the teeth it will attach to, because those teeth are half of the design.

Every peso amount on this page is representative and anchored to published market ranges rather than a fee Elevate has quoted. Your own figure follows the clinical assessment and is quoted per treatment.

What moves the final figure

  • Material and construction. Acrylic, cast cobalt chrome and flexible thermoplastic are priced differently, and the cast case carries an extra try in appointment with it.
  • How many teeth are replaced. Pricing is by the appliance and by the number of units it carries, so a long span costs more than a short one.
  • How many arches. Upper only, lower only, or both, and both is not simply double because the two are designed against each other.
  • Preliminary treatment. Extractions, fillings, crowns on the clasping teeth and periodontal treatment are each quoted separately and none is folded into the headline.
  • Case complexity. An awkward distribution of what is left, a difficult bite, or a requirement that no metal shows at all, each changes the design and the hours it takes.

How you pay

Elevate Dental does not accept HMO or dental insurance. All treatment is self-pay.

HMO coverage cannot be used for dental treatments at our clinics. It is stated in the stage where money is discussed, because finding it out midway through a plan is worse than reading it now.

The figure for your mouth comes from your mouth, so the only route to it is an examination and a quotation you can take away.

Stage 07, the bench and the instruments

Who owns the case

This is prosthodontics, the recognised specialty concerned with replacing missing teeth and restoring the bite, practised here in house as a main clinical discipline.

Where the plan needs crowns on the clasping teeth, or gum treatment before the design is settled, that work is done by a specialist inside the same practice. The case is not handed to another clinic between visits and handed back with a different design.

We name the systems because a name can be checked. An adjective cannot.

iTero intraoral scanner

Records the arches, the teeth that remain and the bite as data. The scan is stored, so a design can be reviewed with you on screen and re-sent to the laboratory without recalling you to sit through another impression.

OccluSense bite analysis

This appliance shares its load between your natural teeth and the ridge, and an uneven bite drives that load onto the wrong one. OccluSense maps where each contact lands and how hard, in sequence, so the adjustment at insertion is made against a reading.

3D CBCT imaging

Available at all four clinics. Used to assess the bone under the teeth that will carry the clasps, and to answer the implant question properly if you want that comparison before choosing something removable.

Stage 08, the end of the route

Questions, in the order they arrive

Three questions people ask before the wax try in, and four they ask once they are wearing it.

Bring the appliance you are wearing now, if you have one, along with any previous radiographs. A worn appliance tells a prosthodontist more in thirty seconds than a description does in ten minutes.

A first appointment produces an examination, a design proposal naming the construction, and a quotation. None of the three obliges you to proceed.

Book an Appointment Prosthodontics

Before it is made

Will the clasps show?

It depends where the gaps are and what the appliance is built from. On a cast case the clasps are metal and can be visible on teeth near the front, though positions are planned to hide them wherever the anatomy allows. A flexible nylon case avoids visible metal entirely. You see and approve every clasp position at the wax try in, before anything is processed.

Will it hurt?

Making it is not surgery and needs no anaesthetic. Any extractions in the preliminary stage are carried out under local anaesthetic. Sore spots in the first fortnight of wearing it are common and are relieved by a short adjustment, so book that adjustment rather than leaving the appliance in a drawer.

Do I wear it at night?

Usually not. Leaving it out overnight gives the gum and the clasped teeth several hours without load or plaque trapped against them. Your prosthodontist will tell you if your case is one of the exceptions, and will tell you why.

Once you are wearing it

How long until I can eat normally?

Start with soft food cut small, and chew on both sides at once so the appliance is not levered off on one side. Most patients manage an ordinary diet within two to four weeks. Hard and sticky foods are the last to come back, and some never fully do.

Will it damage my remaining teeth?

It does not have to, but it raises the stakes. A clasped tooth holds more plaque and carries more load than it did before. Cleaning those teeth daily and keeping your recall appointments is what protects them, and it is the reason stage 04 is a stage rather than a footnote.

How long will it serve?

The ridge underneath keeps changing shape, so this is a maintained appliance rather than a one time purchase. Expect a reline when the fit loosens, an adjustment if a tooth is lost or restored, and a review at least once a year. No fixed term is stated here, because no remake policy has been supplied to state.

Can it be altered if I lose another tooth?

In many cases yes, a tooth can be added to the existing base rather than the whole thing being remade. Bring it in as soon as the tooth is lost, while the shape of the ridge underneath still matches what was recorded.

  • Feature. A removable partial denture, designed around the teeth you keep and quoted only after those teeth have been examined, by a prosthodontics specialist in house.
  • Do. Book the free consultation and take the design proposal and the quotation away with you.
  • Means. You know the cost and the clinician before you commit.
