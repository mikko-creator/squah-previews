What this page covers

Seven stages, and the last one on the list is the one people skip and then regret.

Retention is not an epilogue bolted onto the end of orthodontics. Teeth drift back toward where they began, so holding them is part of the treatment rather than an optional extra sold afterwards.

01
Stage 01, plain English

What braces are, and what they are actually repairing

A bracket is bonded to each tooth and a wire is threaded through them. The wire wants to return to its own shape, and as it does, it carries the teeth with it.

Changing the wire changes the force, and changing the force changes what the teeth do next. That is the whole mechanism, and in principle it has not changed in decades. What has changed is the precision with which the target position is planned and the force is sequenced.

Crowded teeth trap plaque where a brush cannot reach. A deep bite wears the lower incisors down. A crossbite loads teeth sideways in a direction they were not built for.

So this is a mechanical repair as much as a cosmetic one, and that is the honest reason to have it done rather than the one usually advertised.

Appliance 01 Metal

Metal brackets

The smallest of the three and the most efficient at delivering force. Where a case needs a lot of movement in a reasonable time, this is the appliance that does it with the least compromise.

Appliance 02 Ceramic

Ceramic brackets

Tooth colored and less visible. These suit adults who want a fixed appliance, and the reliability that comes with it, without the metal look across the front teeth.

Appliance 03 Self-ligating

Self-ligating brackets

The wire is held by a built-in clip rather than an elastic tie, which changes how the wire is engaged. Which of the three is right for you is a clinical decision made on your records, not a menu choice.

02
Stage 02

Whether this is the right instrument for you

Fixed appliances handle severe rotations and large vertical movements more predictably than any removable appliance, which is why complex cases are still treated this way.

Crowding Spacing Rotated teeth Deep bite Open bite Crossbite A protruding upper arch

Children are usually assessed around age 7, when the first permanent molars and incisors are in. Assessment at that age does not mean treatment at that age. It means the orthodontist can see whether the arches have room, and can time any intervention properly.

The four honest limits

  1. Untreated decay or active gum disease. That is treated before a single bracket goes on. Bonding over inflamed gums or soft enamel makes both worse for two years, and your assessment will say so.
  2. A skeletal problem rather than a dental one. Braces move teeth. They do not move jaws. In a growing child a jaw discrepancy may be treatable with growth modification if it is caught in time. In an adult with a fully grown jaw, a significant discrepancy needs orthognathic surgery planned jointly with an oral surgeon.
  3. Mild to moderate crowding where being visibly in braces is the obstacle. Invisalign (Clear Aligners) is the alternative and it is treated by the same orthodontists here. Aligners are less visible and removable for meals. They are also more dependent on you, because they only work in the hours you actually wear them. Fixed appliances work whether or not you remember them.
  4. An unwillingness to change your brushing routine. Then do not start. Fixed appliances make cleaning harder for the length of the treatment, and the white demineralization marks that follow poor cleaning around brackets are permanent enamel damage. This is said before treatment, not after.

Stage 03, records to retainers

Appointment by appointment

Seven steps, and step 03 is the one most often skipped elsewhere and most often regretted.

Cleaning, fillings, gum treatment or extractions are completed BEFORE any bracket is bonded. Starting a two year appliance on top of unfinished work means carrying that work, and its consequences, for the whole course.

The seven steps, with the time each takes at the chair
StepWhat happensHow long
01 Consultation and records Examination, photographs, radiographs, and a digital scan of both arches. Where root position, impacted teeth or the airway need assessing in three dimensions, 3D CBCT imaging is taken. Your fee and your likely duration are given to you in writing. 60 to 90 minutes
02 Diagnosis and treatment plan The orthodontist sets the target position, decides whether space needs to be created, and decides whether extractions are part of the plan. You see the plan and the reasoning before you agree to it. 1 to 2 weeks
03 Preparatory work Cleaning, fillings, gum treatment or extractions are completed first, in whatever order your mouth requires. Variable
04 Bonding appointment Brackets are bonded and the first wire is placed. There is no anesthesia and no drilling. Expect pressure and soreness for two to five days afterwards. 60 to 120 minutes
05 Adjustments Wires are changed in sequence from light and flexible to heavier and more rigid. Elastics may be added. The soreness after each change is briefer than after the first fitting. 15 to 30 minutes, every 4 to 8 weeks
06 Debonding Brackets and adhesive are removed and the teeth are polished. How long the whole course took depends on how far the teeth had to travel and how consistently you attended. 60 to 90 minutes
07 Retention A fixed retainer, a removable retainer, or both. This step has no end date, and that is not an omission in the table. Indefinite

Stage 04, the bench

Who plans it, and who you see each time

Cases are planned and adjusted by an orthodontics specialist. The diagnosis, the wire sequence and the decision on extractions are theirs.

The same clinician sees you through the case rather than a different dentist at each adjustment. Over two years of monthly visits that continuity is not a courtesy, it is what keeps the plan coherent.

Orthodontics

Owns the case from the first records to the retainer, and carries the decisions no other discipline makes on it.

Oral Surgery

Where a tooth has to come out to create space, or an impacted canine has to be exposed and bonded, the surgeon who does it works in the same practice.

Prosthodontics

Where restorative work follows alignment, it is planned inside the same clinic rather than posted to a third party at the end of the case.

General Dentistry

The cleanings, fillings and gum treatment that step 03 depends on, completed ahead of the appliance rather than squeezed in around it.

Stage 05

The three systems, and when you will not be given one

Three named instruments support an orthodontic case here.

We name the systems because a name can be checked. An adjective cannot.

Station 01

3D CBCT imaging

A flat radiograph shows you a shadow. This shows the root in three dimensions, so an impacted canine can be located precisely, root proximity can be checked before a tooth is moved into a tight space, and resorption risk can be assessed rather than guessed at.

All four clinics
Station 02

iTero intraoral scanner

Both arches are captured digitally, which removes impression trays from the process and leaves a record that can be re-measured later. The same scan supports the clear aligner pathway if you switch to it or finish with it.

Named installed system
Station 03

OccluSense bite analysis

Where the teeth meet at the end of treatment, and how hard, is recorded rather than eyeballed on carbon paper. Alignment that looks correct and loads unevenly is a case that relapses, so this is checked before debonding.

Named installed system

And when you will not be given one

Which of these is used depends on your case. A straightforward crowding case does not need a CBCT scan, and you will not be given one to justify the equipment.

Stage 06, how the number is built

What it costs

From ₱90,000 An indicative market figure, not a quote from Elevate

Full treatment. Anchored to published market ranges, not to an Elevate price list.

Every peso amount on this page is representative and anchored to published market ranges. Your own figure is set after a clinical assessment.

What moves the final figure

  • Case complexity. A mild alignment case finishing in twelve months and a full case with extractions, elastics and a thirty month arc are different pieces of work, and they are priced as such.
  • Appliance type. The three bracket types set out in stage 01 sit at different costs.
  • Number of arches and number of visits. A single arch case is not priced as a double arch case, and a case that runs long runs through more adjustment appointments.

The exact figure is confirmed at consultation, after your records have been taken and read, and it is given to you in writing before treatment starts.

The written figure states what it includes and what it does not.

How you pay

Elevate Dental does not accept HMO or dental insurance. All treatment is self-pay.

HMO coverage cannot be used for dental treatments at our clinics. Payment is by cash, bank cheque, bank transfer, debit or credit card, or QR payment. It is stated here, in the stage where the money is discussed, because finding it out late is worse than reading it now.

A timeline and a fee both come out of records that have actually been read, so the only way to get either is an examination.

Stage 07, the end of the route

Questions, upkeep, and holding the result

Three questions people ask before they start, and four they ask while they are wearing the appliance.

Before you start

How long will I be in braces?

Most cases run between 12 and 30 months. The orthodontist gives you an estimated range at the planning stage, based on your actual records rather than on an average. Missed appointments extend it, and so does a bracket that keeps coming loose.

Does it hurt?

Fitting itself is not painful and needs no anesthetic. The soreness comes afterwards: pressure and tenderness on biting for two to five days after the fitting, and a shorter, milder version after each adjustment. Soft food and ordinary analgesia handle it, and orthodontic wax covers a bracket that is rubbing.

Braces or clear aligners?

It depends on the movements required and on you. Fixed appliances handle severe rotations, large vertical corrections and extraction cases more predictably. Aligners are less visible and removable, and they suit mild to moderate cases in patients who will wear them 20 to 22 hours a day. The same orthodontists treat both here, so the recommendation is not steered by what the clinic happens to offer.

While you are wearing it, and after

What can I not eat?

Nothing hard, sticky or stringy. No ice, no nuts, no hard bread crusts, no sticky candy, and no biting straight into apples or corn on the cob. Cut hard food into pieces. A broken bracket costs you an unscheduled appointment and adds time to the case.

How do I clean around brackets?

Brush after every meal, angling the brush above and below the bracket. Use an interdental brush around each bracket and a floss threader or superfloss between the teeth once daily. This is the single behavior that decides whether you finish with straight teeth or straight teeth with white marks on them.

Will my teeth move back afterwards?

Yes, if you stop wearing retainers. Teeth are held in bone that remodels for months after treatment, and the tissue fibers around them keep pulling toward the original position for longer than that. Retainers are worn nightly, indefinitely. That is not a sales line, it is the condition on which the result holds.

Can I have this with crowns, bridges or implants?

Crowns and bridges can usually be bracketed, with adjustments to the bonding technique. An implant cannot be moved: it is fused to bone and stays exactly where it was placed, so it becomes a fixed point the plan has to work around. This is why the orthodontist should see you before an implant is placed, not after.

Orthodontic cases are recorded here in photographs and scans at the start, during treatment and at debonding, and those records are not published on this page. Publishing an identifiable patient's teeth requires that patient's documented written consent.

Upkeep, during and after

  • Attend every scheduled adjustment.
  • Call the clinic if a bracket comes loose or a wire is poking, rather than waiting for the next appointment.
  • Wear your elastics for the hours you are told, because the mid-treatment correction depends on them entirely.
  • After debonding, wear your retainers exactly as instructed.
  • Bring the retainers to every recall so they can be checked for fit and wear.

The first consultation is free. You will be examined, your records taken and read by an orthodontist, and you will be told honestly whether a fixed appliance, clear aligners, or a joint plan with another specialist is the right answer for your case. Confirm current consultation terms with the clinic before you book.
