Endodontics treats the living tissue inside a tooth.

02
What this field covers

What brings a tooth here

The pulp is the nerve and blood supply that runs through the center of a tooth. Deep decay, a fracture or a heavy knock can let bacteria reach it. Once that happens the pulp does not recover on its own, and the problem moves down the root towards the bone.

  • Lingering ache. A toothache that stays after heat or cold is taken away, or that wakes you at night.
  • Tender to bite on. One tooth that objects when you put pressure on it.
  • A swelling. An abscess or a swelling in the gum near a root tip.
  • A darkened tooth. One that has changed color after an injury.
  • Unsettled work. A previous treatment that has not settled and needs redoing.
  • A crack. A cracked tooth where the pulp has been exposed.
  • Before a crown. A tooth that needs the pulp removed before a crown can be built over it.

Treatment is carried out under local anesthesia, and the tooth is isolated so the canal stays clean while it is being worked on.

What happens next, and where

The step after this one is in the same building.

Endodontics is one of the nine fields of dentistry practiced in-house at Elevate Dental. If the tooth needs a crown afterwards, the prosthodontist is in the same clinic. If the tooth turns out to be unrestorable, the oral surgeon is too. You are not sent elsewhere between the diagnosis and the repair.

What the table cannot decide

Which row your tooth belongs to is a reading, not a preference. How much sound structure is left, how far the infection has travelled and what the root looks like in three dimensions are what choose, and all three are read before anything is drilled.

A tooth that has had this treatment usually needs a crown afterwards to protect what is left of it. That is a separate treatment with a separate fee, listed on the price page.

05
Technology used here

The imaging decides the plan

A molar can carry four or more canals, some of them curved, some hidden behind a ledge of dentine. What is known before the tooth is opened comes from two systems.

01

3D CBCT imaging

A periapical film is a flat picture of a three-dimensional problem. A cone-beam volume shows how many canals a tooth has, how they curve, and how far infection has spread at the root tip, before anything is opened. Available at all four clinics.

02

Digital radiography

Digital sensors are used during treatment to confirm working length and to check the final seal. The image appears immediately, so the check happens while you are still in the chair rather than at a second appointment.

Where the volume is taken

The cone-beam scan is available at Mitsukoshi BGC, Shangri-La Plaza, Bonifacio Stopover BGC and Alabang Town Center, so the reading that decides your plan does not wait on an outside imaging center.

The specialists in this field

Why repetition is the whole argument

A molar can carry four or more canals and their shape is not fully known until it is reached. An endodontist works on that anatomy every day.

That is the case for keeping this work in-house. A general practice that meets a difficult molar occasionally sends it to somebody else, and you start again with a clinician who has not met your case. Here the assessment, the treatment and the restoration that follows it stay with one team.

Every specialist profile carries a full name, the field that clinician practices and their postnominal credentials, so you can read who will treat you before you book rather than after you arrive.
