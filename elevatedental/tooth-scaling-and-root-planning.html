Stage 01, the mechanism

What a pocket is

Gum disease starts as a soft bacterial film at the gum margin. Left there, it hardens into calculus.

The gum then detaches from the tooth, and a space opens between them. That space is the pocket. Inside it the deposit sits directly on the root surface, out of reach of any toothbrush, and the body answers the bacteria by breaking down the bone holding the tooth in place.

The treatment has two halves and they are different jobs. Scaling lifts the calculus and the biofilm off the tooth and the root. Root planing then smooths the root surface the deposit was gripping, so the gum tissue can reattach against something clean and the pocket can shrink.

It is carried out under local anaesthetic, usually one quadrant or one half of the mouth at a time, and it is measured before and afterwards, so what you are given at the end is a number rather than an impression.

The bone that has already gone is not restored by this treatment. What it does is stop the loss and let the tissue reattach against a clean root.

Not the routine clean

A routine oral prophylaxis and this are different treatments for different mouths, and being clear about which one you need is worth money to you.

If your gums are inflamed but no bone has gone, that is gingivitis, and a prophylaxis with a change in your daily cleaning usually resolves it. We will say so, and we will book the shorter appointment.

Stage 03

The signs, and the measurement

You are likely to need this if you recognise more than one of the following.

Gums that bleed when you brush or floss Gums that are red and swollen Breath that has changed A tooth that has loosened or drifted Gums receding, teeth looking longer

It is also the treatment that goes first when other work is planned. Crowns, bridges, implants and orthodontics all rest on gum and bone, so the disease is stabilised before any of them is started.

How a suspicion becomes a number

Periodontal charting is the measurement. The depth of the pocket is recorded at six points around every tooth, alongside bleeding scores, mobility and recession, and radiographs record the bone level. Depths beyond about three millimetres that also bleed are the usual threshold at which this treatment is indicated. That chart is your baseline, and it is the thing stage 09 compares against.

Stage 04, the limits

Where this is not enough on its own

Cleaning a root surface is a mechanical act, and an instrument can only go where its shape allows.

Turning 01 Too shallow to need it

Inflammation, but no bone lost

Red, swollen, bleeding gums with the bone still intact is gingivitis, not periodontitis. A routine clean and a real change in daily technique resolves most of it, and being sold the deeper treatment for it is being sold something you do not need.

Oral prophylaxis
Turning 02 Beyond an instrument

Pockets an instrument cannot follow

Where a pocket is deep, or the bone has gone in a pattern with corners a curette cannot reach into, this treatment will improve the situation without closing the pocket. Periodontal surgery, regenerative treatment or grafting is then the honest next step, and it is a decision made at the re-evaluation rather than guessed at the start.

Turning 03 Past saving

Advanced loss, and a tooth that moves

A tooth with very advanced bone loss and real mobility may not be savable at all. We will say that rather than treat around it and charge you for the treating. The conversation then moves to removal and to what replaces it, which is a separate plan with a separate quote.

Stage 05, outside the building

The two factors we do not control

Two things in your own life change this outcome more than anything we do at the chair.

Smoking reduces how the tissue responds, and it constricts the small vessels in the gum so it also masks the bleeding that would otherwise have warned you early. A smoker's gums can look calmer than a non smoker's while the bone underneath is going faster.

Uncontrolled diabetes does much the same to the healing response, and the relationship runs both ways: treated gum disease can make glycaemic control easier, and poor control makes gum disease harder to hold.

Neither is a reason to withhold treatment and neither is a judgement. They are reasons to change the plan and to shorten the recall interval, which can only happen if we know. Say so at the consultation.

No success rate, percentage of pockets closed, or period of stability is claimed for this treatment. What is promised is a measurement and an honest reading of it - a commitment to the process, not to an outcome.

If you are working on stopping, say that too. It changes the sequencing conversation, because tissue that is about to start responding better is worth measuring again at a different interval.

Stage 06, baseline to maintenance

The six appointments

Most cases run to two to four treatment visits over two to six weeks, and then the one that decides everything.

The plan handed to you at the consultation states the number of visits for your mouth rather than for a typical one, because the number follows the charting and the charting has already happened by then.

Step 01 45 to 60 minutes

Consultation and periodontal charting

Depths recorded at six positions on each tooth, plus bleeding scores, mobility, recession and radiographs of the bone level. This is the baseline. The written quote is handed over at the same appointment.

Step 02 15 to 20 minutes

Hygiene instruction, at the same visit

Which interdental brush size fits which space, and how to actually use it. This treatment does not hold without this part, so it is taught with brushes in your hand rather than mentioned on the way out.

Step 03 One to four visits

The treatment itself

Local anaesthetic for the area being worked on, then the root surfaces inside each pocket are cleaned and smoothed with ultrasonic and hand instruments. Quadrants or halves, so each area gets adequate time.

Step 04 At four to six weeks

Interim review

The tissue is checked for early healing and any deposit still present is dealt with. It is a short visit and it is not the judgement visit.

Step 05 At six to eight weeks

Re-evaluation

The full chart is repeated and set against the baseline. This is where the work is judged: which pockets closed, which improved, and which need something more. Stage 09 is about reading it.

Step 06 Every three to four months

Maintenance recall

This disease is controlled rather than cured, and the interval between maintenance visits is the strongest single predictor of whether it stays controlled. The interval is set shorter than the time the film needs to re-establish.

Stage 07, the bench and the instruments

Who does this work

This is periodontics, the recognised specialty concerned with the gum and the bone that support the teeth, practised here in house as a main clinical discipline.

If the re-evaluation shows surgery, grafting or a removal and replacement is needed, that next stage is handled by a specialist inside the same practice rather than by a referral out to somebody who has never seen your baseline.

We name the systems because a name can be checked. An adjective cannot.

Our Specialists Periodontics
System 01

AirFlow (guided biofilm therapy)

A controlled jet of warm water, air and a fine erythritol powder that lifts bacterial biofilm and staining off the tooth, off the root surface inside the pocket, and from around implants and orthodontic attachments. It follows contours a hand instrument cannot, and it is gentler on the root than repeated hand scaling.

Named installed system
System 02

Piezotome

A piezoelectric ultrasonic unit. Its motion breaks up hardened deposit efficiently on the root surface, and in periodontal surgery the same technology cuts bone while leaving the soft tissue beside it intact.

Named installed system
System 03

Sol Dental Laser

Used as an adjunct inside the treated pocket to decontaminate the soft tissue wall. It works alongside the mechanical cleaning rather than instead of it, and no page here will tell you a laser removes the need for the instruments.

Named installed system
System 04

3D CBCT imaging

Used where the PATTERN of the loss decides the plan: a defect wrapping one root, a furcation between the roots of a molar, or an assessment of whether a site could take an implant if the tooth turns out not to be keepable.

How you pay

Elevate Dental does not accept HMO or dental insurance. All treatment is self-pay.

HMO coverage cannot be used for dental treatments at our clinics. It sits in the stage where money is discussed, because discovering it partway through a course of treatment is worse than reading it here.

What moves the final figure

  • How much of the mouth is involved. This is priced by quadrant or by half, so a localised case and a whole mouth case are simply different quotes rather than a discount off one another.
  • Depth and deposit. Deeper pockets and heavy hardened deposit take more chair time per area, and time is what is being bought.
  • How many visits. Some courses finish in two appointments and others need four.
  • Imaging. Radiographs and, where the pattern of loss requires it, a scan, are quoted as their own lines.
  • What follows. Surgery, grafting or extractions identified at the re-evaluation are separate treatments with separate quotes, and none of them is folded into this one.

Every peso amount on this page is representative and anchored to published market ranges - it is not a price quoted by Elevate Dental. Your own figure is quoted per treatment after a clinical assessment.

The charting is what determines the work, so the appointment that measures it is the appointment that produces your figure.

Stage 09, judged by measurement

How it is judged, and what stays lost

This treatment is judged by measurement, which is unusual in dentistry and useful to you.

Your baseline records a depth and a bleeding score at six positions on each tooth. Six to eight weeks after the work, the same points are measured again and the two charts are set side by side in front of you. You do not have to take anybody's word for whether it improved.

What improvement looks like: depths that have reduced, bleeding scores that have fallen, and firmer tissue that no longer bleeds when you clean between the teeth. What it does not look like is bone that has grown back. This stops further loss and lets the tissue reattach against a clean root. It does not rebuild what has already gone, and any page telling you otherwise is selling something.

Expect the gums to shrink a little as the swelling resolves. That can leave slightly more root exposed, the teeth looking longer, and small spaces opening between them. That is healed tissue rather than a complication, and it is much better known in advance than discovered in a mirror.

Stage 10, the end of the route

Questions, and the part that is yours

Eight answers, and one daily technique that matters more here than on any other treatment page on this site.

Every day, at home

  • Brush twice a day for two minutes, angled at the gum margin rather than scrubbed across the middle of the teeth.
  • Clean between every tooth once a day, with the interdental brush sizes you were given rather than the ones on offer at the chemist.
  • Keep the maintenance interval you were set, even in the months when everything feels fine, because feeling fine is what this disease does.
  • If you smoke, stopping does more for the result than anything else on this list, including anything done at the chair.

Bring previous radiographs and a list of your medication, including anything that affects bleeding. The first appointment includes the full charting, the plan and the written quote.

Will it hurt?

Local anaesthetic is given for each area before the work starts, so the cleaning itself is done on a numb quadrant. Afterwards the gums are usually tender for a day or two, and the teeth can be sensitive to cold for a few weeks while the tissue settles. Ordinary painkillers taken as directed are enough for most people.

Why does it take more than one visit?

Cleaning a pocket properly takes time, and doing a whole mouth in one sitting means either rushing it or numbing everything at once. Working in quadrants or halves gives each area the minutes it actually needs.

Will my teeth feel loose afterwards?

They can feel slightly different once the swelling settles, because inflamed tissue had been holding them snugly. Genuine mobility is assessed at the re-evaluation and is a finding to act on rather than something to wait out. Tell your periodontist if a tooth is moving.

Will the sensitivity go away?

Usually. Exposed root surface is sensitive while it settles and commonly improves over two to six weeks. A desensitising toothpaste used consistently helps, and a fluoride application at the clinic can be added if it persists.

Do I need antibiotics?

Not routinely. The deposit on the root is a physical film, so removing it mechanically is the treatment. Antibiotics and antiseptic rinses are used selectively, in aggressive cases or alongside surgery, and they are prescribed rather than assumed.

How often will I need maintenance?

Every three to four months for most patients, at least until the tissue has been stable for a sustained period. Stretching that back out to a six monthly recall is the most common reason treated gum disease comes back.

Can I have this done while pregnant?

Tell us you are pregnant when you book, and the timing, the imaging and the anaesthetic are planned around it. Gum inflammation commonly worsens in pregnancy, so it is a conversation to have rather than a reason to postpone indefinitely.

Which clinic can I book?

There are four clinics in Metro Manila: Mitsukoshi BGC, Shangri-La Plaza, Bonifacio Stopover BGC and Alabang Town Center. Which of them schedule this work, and on which days, is still being confirmed with the practice, so book through the appointment control and you will be placed at the branch that can take your case.
