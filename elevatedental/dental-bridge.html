Stage 01 opens with the anatomy: which unit replaces the missing tooth, which units do the carrying, and why it is set in place as a single object.

Open stage 01
01
Stage 01, plain English

What a dental bridge is

A bridge is one piece of dental work made of several joined units.

The false tooth in the middle is the pontic. The units on either side are crowns that sit over your own teeth, and those teeth carry the load. The whole span is cemented as a single restoration, which is why it cannot be taken out and why this work is counted by how many units it has rather than by how many teeth it replaces.

Material decides both the look and the strength. Zirconia is the usual choice where chewing force is highest, at the back of the mouth. Lithium disilicate, sold as Emax, is used at the front where translucency matters and light has to pass through the way it passes through enamel. Porcelain fused to metal remains a durable option in specific cases. Your prosthodontist will tell you which one your case calls for, and why, before any tooth is shaped.

The problem this solves is a gap that has begun to affect the rest of the mouth. Teeth on either side of a space tilt into it, the opposing tooth drifts down to meet nothing, food packs into the void, and the bite changes. Closing the space stops that sequence and gives you back chewing on that side.

The honest part

Fitting a conventional bridge means reducing the two teeth beside the gap so the crowns fit over them without leaving the teeth bulkier than they were. That reduction is irreversible. From then on those teeth need a crown on them. It is the trade you are making for a fixed replacement with no surgery, and it is the first thing discussed at the assessment rather than the last.

Stage 02

Whose gap a bridge should close

This suits you if you are missing one tooth, or a short run of them, and the teeth on both sides of the space are healthy, well held by bone, and either already crowned or heavily filled.

One missing tooth A short run of two or three Neighbours already crowned Neighbours heavily filled A fixed result without surgery An implant ruled out medically

If those neighbours already need crowns, the work does two jobs at once, which is the case where this treatment is at its most efficient. If one of the three turnings below describes your mouth instead, take it, and read the treatment named on it next.

Turning 01 Leave them alone

A single implant instead

If the two teeth flanking the space are sound, untouched and free of fillings, a single implant is usually the better treatment, because it puts a tooth back without cutting into two healthy ones to hold it there. Trading two intact teeth for a fixed span is a real cost, and it is counted here rather than passed over.

Dental implant
Turning 02 Treat the gums

Periodontal care comes first

A span loads two teeth with the work of three. If gum disease has already taken bone from around them, they cannot be asked to carry that extra share, and cementing anything onto them shortens their life instead of lengthening it. The gums are treated and stabilised first, and the design is planned afterwards against what the bone will actually hold.

Periodontics
Turning 03 Too long a reach

A long gap needs another design

Ceramic flexes across distance, and a span stretched over a long empty stretch will flex, then fail, and take a support tooth with it when it goes. An implant supported solution or a removable partial denture is the honest alternative in that situation, and which of the two fits is a prosthodontic decision made with your radiographs in front of you.

What we will tell you

If your case is not one this treatment should take, you will be told so at the consultation and shown which of the three turnings applies. Active decay is dealt with before any of them, on whichever path the case finally goes down.

Stage 03, assessment to recheck

How the treatment runs

Seven visits, in this order, with how long each one holds the chair.

Two of them are gates rather than steps. Nothing is shaped until the bone around your own teeth has been read on a radiograph and the design has been agreed, and nothing is cemented until the fit, the contacts and the shade have been checked with the work still removable. Those two gates are why the list is as long as it is.

The seven visits and what each is for
VisitWhat happensHow long
01 Consultation and assessment Clinical examination, radiographs, and a judgement on whether the teeth flanking the space, and the bone holding them, can take the extra share. Material and design are agreed. You receive the written quote at this appointment. 45 to 60 minutes
02 Preliminary work, where needed Decay, a failing filling, root canal treatment or gum treatment on a support is completed before anything else starts. This is quoted separately, and not every case needs it. Case by case
03 Preparation and records The two flanking teeth are numbed and shaped to receive their crowns. The arch is scanned or impressed, the bite is recorded, and the shade is matched. 90 to 120 minutes
04 Temporary fitted, same sitting A provisional is cemented lightly so the shaped teeth are protected and the space is not visible while the laboratory works. Same appointment
05 Laboratory fabrication The units are built and joined to the scan and the bite record. Nothing happens at the chair during this period. One to two weeks
06 Try-in and cementation Fit at the margins, the contact points, the shade and the bite are all checked before cement is mixed. Adjustments belong at this stage, not after. 45 to 60 minutes
07 Review Your bite is read again once you have been using it, and you are shown how to clean the underside of the span. One short visit, two to four weeks later

Your calendar

Most cases finish in two to three appointments across two to four weeks. A case needing endodontic or periodontal work first takes longer, and the plan handed to you at the consultation states the sequence for your mouth specifically.

Stage 04, one discipline

Who performs it

Bridgework is prosthodontics, and prosthodontics is a recognized dental specialty, not a word a general practice can award itself.

The specialty exists to replace missing teeth and rebuild a bite, and at Elevate Dental it is practised in house by clinicians who do this as their main clinical discipline rather than as an occasional job between other work.

If your case needs endodontic care on a carrying tooth first, or the gums stabilised before the design is committed to, that is handled by a specialist inside this same practice. You are not sent out and back, and no part of your plan is decided by somebody who will never see the finished work in your mouth.

Every clinician is listed by name, discipline and training on the specialists index, filtered to the discipline that will take your case. Read the profiles, then book with the one you want.

Stage 05

The technology behind it

Three systems touch this treatment. They are named here because a name can be checked, and an adjective cannot.

iTero intraoral scanner

A digital scan replaces the impression tray for most of these cases. It records the shaped teeth, the opposing arch and the way they meet in one sitting, and the file leaves for the laboratory the same day rather than travelling as a physical mould that can distort on the way.

OccluSense bite analysis

Joining teeth together changes how force moves through them, because one high contact is no longer absorbed by one tooth, it is carried by the whole joined piece. OccluSense maps where every contact lands and how hard, in sequence, so the finished work is adjusted against a reading instead of against a strip of articulating paper alone.

3D CBCT imaging

Available at all four clinics. The scan shows bone level and root anatomy in three dimensions, which is how the teeth being asked to carry the load are checked before they are committed to. It is also how the implant comparison is assessed, if you want that answered properly before you decide.

The one to two week fabrication window shown here is a market-typical range, not a figure quoted by this practice or its laboratory.

Stage 06, how the number is built

What it costs

₱23,000 Representative of the market, not an Elevate price

The starting figure, per unit.

The amount above is a market-derived figure, not a price quoted by this practice. The figure for your own case is quoted after a clinical assessment.

What moves the final figure

  • How many units. The fee is built per unit, so a three-unit span replacing one tooth costs more than a single crown does.
  • Material. Zirconia, lithium disilicate and porcelain fused to metal are priced differently from one another.
  • Preliminary work. Root canal therapy, a core build-up, or periodontal care on either abutment is quoted as separate work.
  • Design. A conventional span, a cantilever and a resin-bonded design are three different pieces of laboratory work.
  • Complexity. Front teeth, a difficult shade match and an altered bite each add clinical time.

Your exact figure is confirmed in writing at the consultation, after the examination and the radiographs. No quote is given for this treatment without seeing the teeth that would carry it, which is why nothing here is offered as a phone estimate.

How you pay

Elevate Dental does not accept HMO or dental insurance. All treatment is self-pay.

HMO coverage cannot be used for dental treatments at our clinics. It is stated here, in the stage where money is discussed, because learning it late, halfway through a plan, is worse than reading it now.

Your own number comes from your own mouth, so the only way to reach it is an examination and a quote you can hold.

Stage 07, the standard of care

The check that decides it

Work adjusted at try-in and work adjusted after cementing are two different standards, and only one of them can still be sent back.

At the try-in, the seal at the margins, the contact against each adjacent tooth, the shade in daylight and the way the piece meets the opposing arch are all assessed while the laboratory can still take it away and correct it. That is the moment worth insisting on, and it is why the sixth visit is scheduled with time in it rather than squeezed in at the end of a list.

The second check is the review. Once you have chewed on it for a fortnight, your bite is read again, because a restoration that felt correct in the chair settles differently in use, and a contact that is fractionally high is easier to relieve at three weeks than to explain at three years.

No result is described to you before your own mouth has been examined. What this treatment will look like, how much of the space it can close, and how the shade will sit against the teeth you keep are all answered on your own case, at the assessment.

No durability period, free-replacement window or repair commitment is stated on this page.

Stage 08, eight answers

Questions, grouped by when they arrive

Two asked before consenting, two asked while the provisional is in, and four asked in the years afterwards.

None is hidden behind a form, and none is answered with a figure this page cannot stand behind.

Treatment Prices Our Specialists

Before you consent

Does the tooth next door have to be cut down?

Yes, for a conventional design. Each flanking tooth is reduced so its crown fits over it without leaving the tooth fatter than it started. This is the single reason the implant option is put in front of you first whenever those neighbours are still untouched.

Bridge or implant?

One is faster and involves no surgery, but it commits two teeth that are currently doing nothing wrong. The other takes longer and has a surgical stage, but it leaves those two alone. Both are done in house here, so the recommendation you get is the one your radiographs support rather than the one the practice happens to sell.

While the provisional is in

Will it hurt?

The shaping appointment is carried out under local anesthetic. Cold sensitivity for a few days afterwards, while the provisional is on, is common, and it settles once the definitive piece is cemented. Tell your clinician if it does not settle, rather than waiting for the review.

How long am I in a temporary?

One to two weeks in most cases. It looks and works well enough to eat and speak normally, but it is cemented lightly on purpose so it can be lifted off again, so avoid very hard or sticky food while it is on and call us if it loosens.

In the years after

How long will it last?

That depends on your bite, on the health of the two teeth doing the carrying, and on how well the underside is cleaned. This kind of work fails at those teeth far more often than it fails at the porcelain, which is why the cleaning routine matters more than the material does. No fixed term is quoted here, because no remake policy has been supplied.

How do I clean underneath it?

You cannot pass floss between the units, because they are joined. Use a threader or superfloss to draw floss beneath the false tooth once a day, and an interdental brush wherever the space allows one. Two minutes a day there is what keeps the carrying teeth healthy.

Can it be whitened later?

No. Porcelain and zirconia do not respond to whitening agents. If you are thinking about lightening the teeth you still have, do that first and let the shade settle, then have the new work matched to the result. The order is not optional.

What if a carrying tooth fails years later?

The piece has to come off, the tooth is treated or replaced, and the span is redesigned around what is left. That is precisely why those teeth are assessed so carefully at the start instead of being worked around when one of them is already doubtful.

Stage 09, the end of the route

Aftercare, and what your first visit hands you

Joined units are looked after the way natural teeth are, with one addition that is not optional.

  • Treat the provisional gently: softer food, no lifting floss straight up and out at its edge, and a phone call rather than a wait if it comes loose.
  • Once the definitive work is in place, pass floss under the pontic daily with a threader or superfloss. Nothing else reaches that surface, and that surface is where trouble starts.
  • Add an interdental brush wherever the gum has left room for one, and use it at the joints rather than only at the ends.
  • Do not use your front teeth as tools. No ice, no fingernails, no tearing packaging open.
  • If you grind at night, wear the guard every night, because a joined piece transmits that force to two teeth instead of one.
  • Keep your recall visits so the margins and the gum levels are read before a small problem becomes a remake.

Bring any previous radiographs and a list of your current medication. The first appointment produces three things: an examination of the teeth and gums against what you want changed, a treatment plan naming the material and the design, and the fee set down in writing. None of them commits you to anything, and none of them requires a tooth to be shaped first.
