02
How this is organised

Field first, treatment second

This site is arranged the way the clinic is arranged.

A field of dentistry is not a marketing category. It is a separate training path with its own specialists, its own instruments and its own way of reading a case. What each one is for, in a sentence:

  • General dentistry keeps a healthy mouth healthy.
  • Endodontics saves a tooth that is already infected.
  • Periodontics treats the gum and bone that hold teeth in place.
  • Prosthodontics replaces what has been lost.
  • Orthodontics moves teeth into position.
  • Oral surgery removes and rebuilds.
  • Pediatric dentistry looks after children on their own terms.
  • Cosmetic dentistry changes how a smile reads.
  • Digital radiography sees what none of the others can see from the outside.
Why the structure is worth reading

Complex work should not mean being sent somewhere else.

Each field has a hub page. The hub explains the field in plain language, lists every treatment inside it, states what that treatment starts at, and routes you to the people who perform it. A case that needs an oral surgeon and then a prosthodontist is handled by one bench of 31 specialists under one roof, working from a single set of records, in the clinic you already chose.

04
If something is wrong

Name the symptom, not the discipline

You do not have to know which field you need. Describe what your mouth is doing and the field follows from it.

  • A tooth aches, throbs at night, or hurts under pressure. Decay that has reached the nerve is an endodontic problem, and a three-dimensional scan shows how far it has gone before anything is opened.
  • A tooth is sensitive to cold, or has a visible hole, but does not throb. Caught at this stage it is usually one appointment and one filling.
  • Your gums bleed when you brush, or have started to pull back. Bleeding is not normal, and receding gums are a bone problem long before they are a cosmetic one.
  • A tooth is missing, broken at the gum line, or already extracted. A crown, a bridge or a denture rebuilds it; an implant replaces the root first. The consultation decides which, and the two fields plan it together.
  • Your teeth are crowded, gapped or protruding. Braces and clear aligners are both available, and the choice depends on how far the teeth have to move rather than on preference alone.

07
Why your figure will differ

What moves the final fee

A starting figure is a floor, not a quote. Four things lift it, and your dentist can tell you which of them apply once the case has been looked at.

  1. How many units are involved. Veneers, crowns and implants are quoted per tooth, so the count in your design changes the total more than the material does.
  2. Which material is chosen. This is why a composite veneer and a zirconia veneer are separate lines rather than one range.
  3. How complex the case is. A graft, an extra scan, or a handover to a second specialist inside the group all sit inside the plan and inside the figure.
  4. How many appointments the plan takes. Sequenced work is quoted as a sequence, and you see the whole of it before the first one is booked.

Your exact figure is confirmed in writing at the consultation, before anything is scheduled.

Every figure printed on this page is anchored to published Philippine market ranges rather than to an Elevate Dental price list. The figure that applies to you is quoted per treatment after a clinical assessment.

The equipment behind all of it

Eight systems, named so you can ask about them

Named equipment is equipment you can check, question and hold a practice to. These eight sit behind the twenty treatments above.

01

3D CBCT imaging

A layered view of teeth, jawbone, nerve canals and sinuses, used to plan implants, extractions and work inside the root.

02

iTero intraoral scanner

High-resolution digital impressions taken in minutes with a handheld wand, with no tray and no impression putty.

03

RayFace facial scanner

A scan of your face, so veneers and smile design are planned against your own proportions rather than against an average.

04

OccluSense bite analysis

Digital measurement of where and how hard your teeth actually meet, used to check crowns, veneers and finished aligner cases.

05

Sol Dental Laser

Precise soft-tissue work and gum contouring, with less bleeding during the appointment.

06

Piezotome

Ultrasonic instrumentation that cuts bone while sparing the soft tissue around it, used in surgical extractions and implant site preparation.

07

AirFlow guided biofilm therapy

Cleaning with air, warm water and a fine powder, reaching under the gum margin and around brackets, implants and attachments.

08

Zoom whitening

Light-activated in-clinic whitening completed in a single session, with steps taken to limit sensitivity.

10
Start here rather than at the ladder

The consultation decides which field your case belongs to

A specialist looks at the actual case, explains which field it sits in, and gives you a written quote before anything is scheduled. If the case spans two fields, you meet both, and you do not repeat the story twice.

  1. The examination. Teeth, gums and bite are read together, because a complaint in one of the three is often carried by another.
  2. The images your case needs. Taken and read on screen in the same appointment rather than developed and posted on.
  3. The field, named. You are told which discipline takes the case, and who inside the practice holds it.
  4. The written quote. The sequence and the figure are agreed with you before a single appointment is booked.

Twenty treatments is a lot to read. One appointment is not, and it is the only thing on this page that can tell you which of the nine hubs above is yours.
