Go to stage 01
01
Stage 01, plain English

What a direct veneer is

A direct veneer is composite resin built up on the visible surface of a tooth and shaped in the mouth.

The same tooth-coloured material used for fillings is applied in thin layers, each set with a curing light, then contoured and polished until it reads as enamel rather than as a covering.

The word direct is the whole distinction. A porcelain veneer is made outside the mouth by a laboratory technician and bonded on at a second visit. This one is made in the chair by the clinician, in one sitting, with the result in front of you the entire time. That means shape, length, edge translucency and shade are adjusted while you watch, not approved from a model.

Composite is used to close a small gap between the front teeth, rebuild a chipped edge, mask a discoloured patch, correct a tooth that sits slightly short or narrow, and even out an irregular edge line across the smile.

The part that is hard to undo later

Where the tooth simply needs to be widened, lengthened or resurfaced, composite can often be added with little or no enamel removed. Enamel that is never cut is enamel you still have if you decide to change course later, and that is the single most useful thing this treatment offers a patient who is not certain yet.

Stage 02

Who it suits, and where the route forks

Direct veneers suit a defined, mostly cosmetic problem on healthy teeth.

A broken corner A small gap An uneven edge line One tooth out of shade A peg-shaped lateral incisor

They suit patients who want a change completed in one appointment, and patients who would rather not commit to irreversible tooth reduction yet. They are also a considered choice for younger patients, where the gum line will still change with age and a restoration that can be adjusted or replaced is more sensible than a definitive one.

Turning 01 Harder wearing

Porcelain veneers

Composite is softer than porcelain. It takes up colour at the margins over time, particularly with coffee, tea, red wine and tobacco, and it loses surface gloss and needs repolishing. If you want the most stain-resistant and hardest-wearing option, the honest trade is cost and irreversibility against durability.

Porcelain veneers
Turning 02 Protect first

A night guard, before any shaping

If you grind or clench, an unprotected composite edge will chip. That case needs the grinding addressed, usually with a night guard, before or alongside any cosmetic work rather than after the first repair.

General dentistry
Turning 03 Move first

Straighten, then finish

If the teeth are significantly crooked, rotated or crowded, veneering over the misalignment is the wrong instrument. Material added to a tooth that is out of position makes it bulky rather than straight. Orthodontic treatment first, with Invisalign (Clear Aligners) or braces, then a small amount of composite to finish, gives a result the shell alone cannot.

Clear aligners Braces
Turning 04 Deep discolouration

Masking has a limit

If the discolouration is deep and intrinsic, such as tetracycline banding or a darkened non-vital tooth, thin composite may not mask it. Porcelain, or internal whitening of the non-vital tooth, is assessed instead, and which one is decided on the tooth rather than on a preference.

Teeth whitening

Before any of the four

Active decay and untreated gum disease are treated first. Composite bonds to sound enamel, not to a compromised surface, and a margin placed against inflamed tissue will discolour and fail whatever the sculpting is like.

Stage 03, photographs to polish

How the appointment runs

Seven steps, in this order, with the time each one takes at the chair.

One or two teeth are usually completed in a single appointment of one to two hours. A full set of upper front teeth is normally staged across more than one visit, because sculpting six or eight teeth well cannot be rushed.

The seven steps of a direct case
StepWhat happensHow long
01 Consultation and smile assessment Your teeth are photographed and assessed against your lip line, midline and facial proportions. You say what you want changed in your own words. We say what is achievable with this material and what is not. 30 to 45 minutes
02 Digital records and design A digital scan and a facial scan are taken so the proposed shape is planned against your face rather than against a textbook proportion. 20 to 30 minutes
03 Shade selection Shade is chosen before the teeth are isolated, because teeth dehydrate and lighten under isolation and a shade picked late will read wrong once they rehydrate. About 10 minutes
04 Preparation Often minimal. The enamel surface is cleaned and conditioned. Where a small amount of reduction is genuinely needed to avoid a bulky result, it is discussed and agreed before it is done. 10 to 20 minutes
05 Layering and sculpting Composite is placed and light-cured in layers, with different shades and opacities used to reproduce the way a natural tooth transmits light at the body and at the edge. 30 to 60 minutes per tooth
06 Bite check Occlusion is checked and adjusted, particularly on the biting edges of the upper front teeth, which is where an unadjusted edge chips first. About 10 minutes
07 Contouring and polish Shape is refined and the surface is polished through progressive grits. Polish is what makes this material look like enamel and what slows discolouration at the margin. 20 to 30 minutes

What the plan is built on

Five named systems stand behind a case, and four of them are used before any material is placed.

We name the systems because a name can be checked. An adjective cannot.

System 01

RayFace facial scanner

A three-dimensional scan of your face. Proportions are designed against your actual midline, lip line and facial width, so the work is planned in the context it will be seen in rather than against a chart.

Named installed system
System 02

iTero intraoral scanner

A digital scan of the arch, taken without impression material. It records the starting position, supports the design stage, and gives a reference to compare the finished work against.

Named installed system
System 03

OccluSense bite analysis

A digital map of where and how hard your teeth contact. Used to check the new edges against your real bite, which is what protects a sculpted edge from chipping in the first month.

Named installed system
System 04

Sol Dental Laser

Where the tissue sits low and a tooth looks short because of the gum rather than the tooth, soft-tissue contouring is carried out with the laser before any material is placed.

Named installed system
System 05

3D CBCT imaging

Used where the underlying root or bone position needs to be understood in three dimensions before a cosmetic plan is committed to.

  • How much sculpting each tooth needs, and whether multiple shades and opacities are layered.
  • Whether gum contouring is needed before placement.

  • The digital records taken, and whether a design stage is included.

Stage 07, what the material does

How it wears, and what it forgives

We will not tell you what your result will look like before we have seen your teeth.

A well-executed direct veneer restores the shape and edge of the tooth in one appointment and is finished, polished and in function before you leave.

These are commonly expected to serve for a number of years before they need repolishing, repair or replacement, and how long yours serve depends on your bite, whether you grind, and how much staining food and drink the margins are exposed to.

They are repairable. A broken edge is usually rebuilt chairside, which is not true of the ceramic alternative, and that repairability is a genuine advantage rather than a consolation for choosing it.

Shade, edge position and how much can be changed on your specific case are decided at the assessment. No warranty, replacement or remake terms are stated for this treatment.

Stage 08, the end of the route

Questions, aftercare, and the consultation

The first 48 hours matter more than the rest of the year, and one habit matters more than both.

  • Avoid coffee, tea, red wine, curry and tobacco for the first 48 hours while the surface fully matures.
  • After that, treat them as you would enamel, with one exception: do not use them as tools. No opening packaging, no biting nails, no chewing ice or pens.
  • Brush with a non-abrasive toothpaste and floss daily around the margins.
  • If you grind at night, wear the guard every night rather than on the nights you remember.
  • Come back at your recall so the surfaces can be repolished before discolouration sets into the margin.
How long do they last?

A number of years, with maintenance, rather than a fixed figure. The variables are your bite force, whether you grind, and how much colour the margins are exposed to. They are designed to be repolished and repaired over their life rather than replaced wholesale.

Will my tooth be filed down?

Often very little, and in some cases not at all. Where a small reduction genuinely improves the result, it is explained and agreed before it happens. This is the main reason patients choose this treatment over the ceramic alternative.

Do they discolour?

Composite takes up colour at the margins over time, more readily than porcelain does. Coffee, tea, red wine, dark sauces and tobacco accelerate it. Polishing at your recall visit removes most of what has settled on the surface.

Can they be whitened later?

No. This material does not respond to whitening gel. If you want a lighter overall shade, whiten your natural teeth first and let the shade settle, then match the work to the new shade. Doing it in that order is the difference between a matched result and a mismatched one.

How do they compare to porcelain?

Composite: one visit, conservative, repairable, lower starting price, more prone to discolour and chip. Porcelain: two or three visits, requires irreversible enamel reduction, harder and more stain-resistant, higher starting price. Neither is superior in the abstract. Your teeth, your bite and what you want changed decide it.

Does it hurt?

Where no enamel is removed, anesthetic is usually not needed at all. Where a small reduction is planned, local anesthetic is offered. Tell your clinician if any part of the appointment is uncomfortable and it is adjusted.

Can I have just one tooth done?

Yes, and single-tooth matching is the harder skill of the two. The material has to reproduce the neighbouring tooth's shade, translucency and surface texture, which is why shade selection and layering take the time they do.
