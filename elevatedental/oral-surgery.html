Oral Surgery Philippines. Extractions and Implants.

Below the surface

Oral surgery is the part of dentistry that works below the surface.

Book an Appointment How a case is planned
01

It removes a tooth that cannot be saved, places an implant in the bone that held one, and treats the bone and soft tissue around both. It is planned from a three-dimensional scan rather than a flat image, because what matters is where the nerve, the sinus and the bone actually are.

Here it is performed in the same practice that will restore the tooth afterwards, so the surgical plan and the restorative plan are drawn together.

Half one Out

A tooth that cannot be kept

Too broken or too decayed to restore, buried under bone, wedged tight against the root beside it, or unwilling to come out whole. Removal is a surgical decision made from what a scan shows rather than from what is visible in the mouth.

Half two Back

A space that has to be answered

A gap is not a neutral state. Neighbouring teeth lean into it and the bone that held the root begins to reduce. What fills it, and whether the site needs building up first, is decided at the same consultation as the removal.

02
What this field covers

The surgical layer of your care

Your surgeon assesses the tooth, the bone supporting it and the structures around it, then plans the procedure and the healing that follows it.

  • Teeth too broken or decayed to restore.
  • Impacted wisdom teeth, including those lying against a nerve or against the root next to them.
  • Surgical extraction where a tooth will not come out whole.
  • Dental implants placed in the jaw to replace a missing tooth.
  • Bone grafting where a site has lost the volume an implant needs.
  • Exposure of a buried tooth so an orthodontist can bring it into position.
  • Removal of cysts and lesions in the jaw.

Every procedure is carried out with local anaesthesia, and your surgeon takes you through what to expect during the appointment and through the healing period before you consent to it.

Sedation dentistry is not claimed anywhere on this page. A reader who needs it should ask on the phone rather than infer it from a page.

04
Technology used here

Seeing the anatomy before reaching it

A flat radiograph shows shapes. Surgery needs distances, and three systems supply them.

01

3D CBCT imaging

Shows the nerve canal, the sinus floor and the volume of bone at the site, which is what decides whether a post can be placed directly or the site has to be built up first.

02

Piezotome

Ultrasonic instrumentation that cuts bone selectively and leaves soft tissue intact, used in extractions and in preparing a site.

03

Digital radiography

Low-dose images taken through treatment and at review, on screen in the same appointment rather than developed later.

05
What follows the procedure

Surgery is almost never the whole treatment

An extraction leaves a gap that somebody fills. A post needs a crown made for it. A buried tooth is handed on to be moved into line. Here those handovers happen between colleagues, on one set of images, so the two plans are made together rather than one after the other by strangers.

The gap has to be filled

Prosthodontics

The crown that goes on a post, and the bridge or denture that answers a space when a post is not the right choice. The restorative plan is drawn at the same consultation as the surgical one.

Prosthodontics
A buried tooth has to be moved

Orthodontics

Exposing a tooth that has not erupted is surgical. Bringing it into line afterwards is not, and the two are sequenced as one plan rather than booked as two unrelated courses.

Orthodontics
The site has to be sound

Periodontics

Gum and bone disease around the remaining teeth is treated before a post is placed, because the tissue that failed one root will not treat a replacement any more kindly.

06
Starting prices

Two floors on two different units

A surgical extraction starts at ₱6,000, an indicative market figure, and a single implant at ₱90,000, likewise indicative and not an Elevate price.

Neither is a quote, and they are not comparable with each other: one is priced per tooth and one per post. Surgical fees move with the difficulty of the case rather than with a count of anything, which is why the number you are given comes after the scan and not before it.

  • An impacted tooth is not an ordinary extraction. Anything that has to be sectioned or uncovered before it moves is a longer procedure with more planning behind it.
  • A site that needs grafting is quoted separately from the post that will eventually go into it.
  • The crown is a separate treatment in a separate field, so an implant figure is not the cost of a finished tooth.

Your exact figure is confirmed at consultation, in writing, before anything is scheduled.

07
Before you agree to anything

Have the scan read to you first

You are shown what the scan found, and the plan covers the whole sequence rather than the day of surgery.

A consultation here starts with an examination and a cone beam scan where the case calls for one. Four things are covered before you decide: what the images show, what the procedure involves, what the healing period asks of you, and what happens to the space afterwards.

That last one is the part most often left out elsewhere, and it is the reason this page routes to a restorative field two bands above. A surgical plan that stops at the extraction is half a plan.

Whether the first consultation carries a fee is not stated on this page, and no complimentary visit is claimed.
