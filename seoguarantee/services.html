The sprawl Menus built for the agency

We made the opposite decision.

Most agency service menus are built for the agency, not for the buyer. One full service agency in our researched set publishes twelve separate services in its own navigation, each one a distinct scope with a distinct quote. Nothing on that menu tells you which four you need, which two overlap, or what happens when the SEO recommendation depends on a web build you did not buy.

So the work of assembling a plan lands on you, and you do it before you have any evidence about what works for your business. Then it lands on you again every month, because the coordination between those line items is not on anyone else's job description.

No. You use what your business needs. All six are in the plan whether you use them in month one or month nine, and none of them is billed separately, so there is nothing to decline and nothing to add on later.

Real, and specified. Each of the six service pages lists its actual scope, and the work runs on a monthly cadence with that same team. If you want the detail before you call, read the scope on the individual pages rather than taking this page's word for it.
