Brief 02The Stall

Almost every business we take on arrives with the same three symptoms.

Figure 02

New leads have dried up. Site traffic is low or flat. The visits that do arrive are not turning into sales. That pattern holds across the verticals we work in, from dental and medical spa to law firms, dispensaries, construction and home services, and real estate. Different industries, one job to be done: rank high on Google for the terms that bring buyers, and convert them when they land.

Content is where that job usually stalls, and not because anyone doubts it matters. You already know the pages need writing. The problem is the next part. Nobody inside the business has a spare eight hours a week to write them, and the people who could are the same people running the business. So it slips a month, then a quarter, and the site sits at the size it was two years ago while competitors publish past you.

The obvious fix is to hire out, and that is where the second problem starts. A freelancer who has never worked in your field writes copy that is grammatically fine and factually hollow, and you end up rewriting it yourself, which was the thing you were paying to avoid. Handing it to an agency as a separate engagement means another scope document, another invoice and another party who does not control your rankings. Neither route gives you a page you would put your name on and a keyword strategy behind it.

Brief 03Three Reasons

Three reasons the content work belongs inside the plan rather than beside it.

Rule 01

Mapped to the guaranteed keyword set.

Every brief we write points at a term from the keyword set the guarantee is attached to. That is the whole selection rule. Content is not commissioned because the calendar says four pieces are due, it is commissioned because a specific term needs a page, or an existing page is not strong enough to hold the position we committed to. If a topic cannot be traced back to that set, we do not write it.

Rule 02

Written to convert, not just to fill.

A page that ranks and then loses the reader is a cost, not a result. Every piece is written to answer the question that was typed into Google and to end somewhere specific, which on this site means a call or a next page. Word count is an output of the answer, not a target set before the research.

Rule 03

Included at every tier.

The six modules in the plan are bundled at no extra charge, and content marketing is one of the six. There is no content tier to upgrade into, no per-article rate, and no separate line on the invoice. The entry plan carries it on the same terms as the top plan.

Brief 04Scope

Content marketing is one of six services in every plan.

The other five are search engine optimization, website design and development, reputation management, conversion optimization, and graphic design.

That matters here more than it looks: content is the module that touches all of the others, because the pages it produces are the pages SEO ranks, CRO tests, and design lays out.

Inside the module, five areas of work.

Figure 04
  1. 01

    Keyword to page mapping.

    Every term in the agreed set is assigned to exactly one page, and every page is assigned a job. This is the step most content programs skip, and skipping it is why sites end up with four pages competing for the same term and none of them winning it.

  2. 02

    Briefs.

    Before anything is written, each page gets a brief: the term, the intent behind it, what a reader needs answered, what has to be included to compete with what already ranks, and the action the page ends on. The brief is what makes the writing repeatable rather than dependent on whoever is at the keyboard.

  3. 03

    Long-form and service-page copy.

    Two different jobs. Long-form earns the position for the informational terms and builds the topical depth the commercial pages sit on top of. Service-page copy carries the commercial terms and has to sell, not just inform. We write both, because a site that only has one of them ranks for the wrong half of its market.

  4. 04

    On-page optimization.

    Titles, meta descriptions, heading structure, internal links, image alt text and schema, applied when the page ships rather than retrofitted in a later audit. The team that writes the page and the team that ranks it are the same team, so this is one step instead of a hand-off.

  5. 05

    Refresh cycles for decaying pages.

    Pages lose ground quietly. Rankings slip a position at a time, traffic drifts down, and nothing looks broken until a year has gone. We track the pages that are decaying against the agreed keyword set and rewrite them on a cycle, which is ordinary maintenance and is included the same way the new work is.

Brief 06What It Replaces

What this replaces is content bought as its own retainer line.

Figure 06

In our researched set, one value-positioned agency organizes its entire navigation by individual service. Content Marketing sits there as its own nav item, alongside SEO, paid marketing, website design and development, social media management and reputation management. That is taken from a capture of the agency's own published navigation on 2026-07-28. We do not name it, because the point is the structure and not the company. Six service surfaces, six separate scopes, six things to buy in whatever order you can work out.

Structurally, that means content is a second purchase. It arrives with its own scope to negotiate, its own renewal date, and its own account manager who is not the person accountable for your rankings. When the SEO recommendation is a new page, someone has to decide whether the content budget covers it, and that decision is yours to make, in the middle of a quarter, with no evidence yet about which pages will earn their keep.

Here the model is the opposite one. One monthly retainer, tiered, all-inclusive, with all six modules in it. The content is not quoted, not scoped separately and not renewed separately. When the keyword work says a page is missing, the page gets written, because the writing is already paid for and the same team owns both halves.

We do not publish a savings total for this, and we will not invent one. The claim we make is structural, and every part of it is checkable: bundled rather than a la carte, published rather than quote-gated, one accountable team rather than two.
