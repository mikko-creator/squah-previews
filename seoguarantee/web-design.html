The gapWhy recommendations stall

The recommendation was right. It just never got built.

The businesses that call us are not short of advice. They are short of leads. Traffic is low or flat, the traffic that does arrive converts into nothing, and the pipeline that used to fill itself has stopped. We hear the same account from dental practices, medical spas, law firms, dispensaries, construction and home services businesses, and real estate firms.

So the SEO audit gets commissioned, and it is usually competent. Rebuild these four templates. Cut the load time on the pages that matter. Fix the heading structure. Add the twelve pages that answer what people actually search for. Replace the form nobody finishes. Then that document leaves the SEO agency and lands with whoever owns the site, and the second process starts. A scope. A quote. An approval. A slot in a development queue.

A quarter later the audit is still a document. The retainer has been paid three times, the rankings have not moved, and the reason is not in dispute: the changes the ranking depended on were never made. Nobody behaved badly. The SEO team could only recommend, the developer could only build what was scoped and paid for, and the gap between the two was yours to stand in.

ScopeThe web work in full

What the web work actually covers

  1. 01

    Design.

    Page and template design for the pages that carry your revenue: the home page, the service pages, the location pages, the forms. Designed around what a searcher needs to see to make contact, not around a gallery of layouts.

  2. 02

    Build.

    The design gets implemented, not handed to you as a mockup. Templates, page structure, navigation, internal linking and the technical foundations the ranking work relies on.

  3. 03

    Responsive implementation.

    Every page built to work at phone, tablet and desktop widths, because most of the searches you are trying to win happen on a phone.

  4. 04

    Page speed and Core Web Vitals.

    Load performance, layout stability and responsiveness measured and worked on directly. Google reports these as part of how a page is assessed, and a slow page is also a page a visitor abandons before your offer reaches them.

  5. 05

    Ongoing iteration.

    The site keeps changing for as long as the plan runs. Pages added, templates revised, speed regressions caught, conversion paths adjusted on the evidence.

ReplacesThe separate project

What this replaces: web work raised as a separate project every time

Figure 06

Take apart the standard arrangement and it is the same loop every time the rankings need something. The SEO side identifies a change. The web side raises it as a project. A new scope gets written, a new quote gets issued, a new approval gets chased, and a new wait begins. Three months of that and the change is still pending while the retainer keeps clearing.

The loop is structural, not anyone's fault. Web work sold as a project has a defined scope and an end date, and that is the wrong shape for work that needs to change every month for as long as you are trying to rank. Anything outside the scope has to be re-quoted, so every fix restarts the process that made the last fix slow.

Our model removes the second transaction. It is a monthly retainer in tiered packages, all-inclusive, with the build team already engaged and already paid for inside the plan. A change the plan requires is internal work, so it gets prioritized rather than quoted. This is a statement about how we are structured, not a comparison of anyone's rates, and no figures other than our own appear on this site.
