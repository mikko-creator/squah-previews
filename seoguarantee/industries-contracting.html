From the search to the estimate request.

What we will and will not count as success.

THE STANDARD

The job is won at the estimate request, not at the click.

This trade's conversion event is a form, a photograph, a scope and a callback. It is not an impulse telephone call. Traffic that never turns into a request is not a partial success in contracting, it is nothing, and a report full of sessions is exactly the sort of number we refuse to sell on.

THE TERM

Trade-and-metro phrases are named in the contract before you sign.

We propose the set from competitive research and you sign it. There is no veto and no substitution right. The path section below carries the detail of how the set is put together.

The inversion

A long consideration cycle is precisely why the assessment is monthly. This is the pillar that inverts the usual objection. In a trade where a homeowner takes six weeks between a first search and a signed contract, "give it time" is the easiest sentence in the world for an agency to say and the hardest one for a client to argue with. A monthly check against named phrases removes that argument in both directions: the position is either held this month or it is not, whatever the revenue happens to be doing behind it. Off-target, the billing stops and the work continues.

The search-to-estimate path

Two halves, contracted together, because in this trade they only work as a pair.

Half one, the phrase set. Demand splits four ways.

01

Trade-and-metro phrases

The trade name plus a city or a suburb. The core commercial set, heavily contested, and where the metro's established firms already sit.

02

Job-scoped phrases

A specific scope of work plus a location. Lower volume per phrase, far higher intent, and the tier where your actual specialism separates you from a general listing.

03

Cost and research phrases

What a job costs, how long it takes, what is involved. Very large volume, poor direct conversion, and valuable because they reach a buyer weeks before they are ready to request anything. These are a different job from the commercial set and should be contracted as such rather than blended into one promise.

04

Homeowner against commercial and general-contractor demand

Two audiences sharing a trade name and sharing almost no phrases. A firm serving both is running two sets.

Half two, and it is inside the same agreed fee

The request for an estimate

The conversion work here is concrete and checkable.

  • the request form and how much it demands before it will let somebody submit
  • whether photographs can be attached
  • whether a scope can be described in the buyer's own words
  • how fast a callback is promised and whether that promise is kept
  • the project gallery a homeowner uses to work out whether you build things that look like the thing they want

That is a scope of work. No conversion figure is attached to it.

The two halves map onto three objectives: get traffic, generate high quality leads, convert those leads to sales. In contracting the middle term carries the weight, because the gap between a visit and a lead is where the money is lost.

Generate high quality leadsThe middle term, and in this trade it is the one carrying the weight.

Convert those leads to salesThe third term, and the one your crew and your pricing own.

Five questions

All five answered in full, and nothing hidden.

01

Is a guaranteed ranking a red flag?

The published warnings describe a real pattern and they are worth reading. No one can guarantee where a page lands and we are not going to pretend otherwise. What is guaranteed here is a term of business about when an invoice is owed, not control over a search engine. No search engine approves of, permits or endorses this arrangement.

02

What will you do, and what will you not do?

Done:

  • technical repair of the site
  • a page for each contracted phrase
  • the local profile and its categories
  • internal linking
  • review flow
  • the rebuild of the request form itself

Not done:

  • purchased links
  • private blog networks
  • spun or padded text
  • phrase stacking
  • hidden text
  • fabricated reviews

Any proposal containing one of those has told you what it is.

03

Who chooses the phrases?

We propose them from a read of the current page one and they are named in the contract you sign. No veto, and nothing gets swapped in after signature.

04

My sales cycle is two months. What does a paused month mean when the revenue has not landed yet?

Answered without sleight of hand. The monthly assessment runs against the agreed phrases and their positions, not against your closed jobs. We do not control your bid pricing, your crew capacity or your close rate, and we will not pretend to. That limit is also your protection: the position is checkable this month regardless of where the revenue has got to. Off-target, the work continues and the billing stops.

05

How long?

The 6- and 12-month windows we state are not yet an agreed contract term, and the agreement is assessed month by month throughout. The industry's published realism band is three to six months and the pattern buyers are warned about is page one in 30 days, so a longer stated window is the honesty signal rather than a weakness.
