Qualification

Is your conversion engine underperforming?

Four questions. Answer them against your own business.

  • Do you know which pages your enquiries actually come from?
  • Can you tell which of your key phrases produce calls rather than sessions?
  • Do visitors who reach your service pages ever reach your contact page?
  • Do you know what happens on mobile compared with desktop?

If the answer to most of those is no, the useful work is probably not more traffic yet.

The volume floor

There is a volume floor where this becomes measurable rather than anecdotal: around a thousand visitors a month or more. Below that, a change can look like an improvement for reasons that have nothing to do with the change.

Buyers are told to be sceptical of agencies that promise conversion outcomes, and that advice is sound. We do not promise a conversion rate. The full argument is on guarantee.

Method

How a conversion change is measured.

A figure produced by a method you were told about afterwards is a different thing from a figure produced by a method agreed in advance.

Every conversion change we report carries five things.

  1. What was measured, named explicitly. Enquiries, calls, bookings or sales, never the word performance.
  2. The baseline it is measured against, and the dates of that baseline.
  3. The period the comparison covers.
  4. The tool the measurement was taken in.
  5. The statement that individual results vary.

We publish a conversion figure only with its baseline and its period attached, or we do not publish it.

The honest limit

What is promised, and what is worked on.

Client outcomes ship in one form and only that form: the client named, the metric named, the change stated with the start and end dates it covers, the tool it was measured in, and the client's permission to publish it.

An outcome missing the dates, the method or the permission is not published. It is held back rather than softened into a vaguer version of itself.

The ranking metrics are what the fee is conditioned on. Conversion is worked on continuously and reported honestly, but the billing pause is triggered by the contracted ranking metrics, not by a conversion rate.

Publishing anything else would be describing a term the contract does not contain.

The metrics are named in the contract before you sign, which is what makes delivered checkable rather than arguable.
