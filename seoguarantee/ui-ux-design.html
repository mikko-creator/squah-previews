The ceiling of a ranking

Why the interface decides what a ranking is worth.

The objectives run in a fixed order: get traffic, generate high quality leads, convert those leads to sales. This page is about what happens between the first and the second.

The ceiling

A position produces a visit, and nothing more.

That is the honest ceiling of a ranking. What the visit becomes is decided in the seconds after the click, on a page we either designed for that moment or did not.

The arrival

The person arriving typed something specific.

They had a particular problem, they described it in their own words, and a search engine sent them to you. If the page they land on makes them hunt for the thing they just typed, then the position we are contracted to hold has bought you a bounce.

The buyer

This is the buyer we are built for.

An owner whose competitors are consistently ranking for the phrases that matter to their business. Taking the position is the first half. Being the one they call is the second, and it is the half this page is responsible for.

The promise attached to all of it stays the same one, and it is set out as a term further down this page. The interface is not guaranteed.

Scope

What we design.

Representative service copy, pending confirmation

01

Journeys.

The route from a search result to an enquiry, mapped per page template rather than assumed. A visitor landing on a vertical page has a different starting point from one landing on the home page, and pretending otherwise is how a journey gets designed for nobody.

02

Navigation.

Menu structure and behaviour, including custom dynamic menus and multi-level menus, and the top and bottom panel toolbars that keep the primary action reachable wherever a visitor has scrolled to.

03

Page templates.

What a service page, a vertical page, a location page and a contact page each have to do, and where the single primary action sits on each of them. One action per page, in a place the eye reaches without being told.

04

Enquiry forms.

Field order, length and error behaviour, designed so that the form is the same action as the phone call rather than a competing one. A form that argues with the phone number next to it costs you both.

05

Mobile behaviour.

Thumb reach, tap targets, sticky access to the call, and deciding what collapses rather than letting everything shrink.

06

Client portal.

How you see interface work in progress and request more of it.

One action per page, in a place the eye reaches without being told, is the rule the six groups above are all in service of.

The mechanic

How this is assessed and billed.

  1. A monthly retainer is agreed up front.
  2. Each month is assessed against the ranking metrics named in the contract.
  3. In a month we are off target, the design work continues and your billing stops.
  4. Billing resumes when results are back on track.

We keep working, you stop paying. The obligation is suspended for that month rather than erased.

There is a fair question underneath this, which is why a page about interface design is talking about billing at all. The answer is that the design work is not sold separately. It is carried by a fee that is conditioned on delivery, so the condition belongs on every page the fee covers. And the honest part of the sceptic's case stands: nobody controls how a search engine ranks anything. What is being described here is a term about when an invoice is owed, not a prediction, and the full terms are set out on guarantee.
