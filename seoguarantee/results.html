The standard Three parts, no ranking

The number, the window, the method.

The number

One figure per case, stated once, and identical everywhere it appears on this site. If two pages credit the same client two different numbers, both stop being evidence.

The window

The period the number was measured over. A figure without a window cannot be judged, because a rise from almost nothing over two years and a rise over one quarter are not the same event.

The method

Which of the six modules did the work, and in what order. This is the part a competent buyer actually reads.

We publish all three because what we sell is a committed first-page outcome rather than a monthly log of activity. Every researched agency in this category will report on effort. We are the one attaching the promise to the placement, and a promise like that is only credible if the results behind it can be inspected the same way.

The teardown Including ours

How to take a case study apart, including ours.

A checkable claim has three parts. Remove any one of the three and the claim stops being evidence and becomes tone.

  • Source A source you can open.
  • Window A window the measurement was taken over.
  • Method A method that says what was done.

Here is what the category ships instead, and what each omission hides.

  1. 01A percentage with no start date, which could be a strong quarter or a site that began at zero and had nothing to lose.
  2. 02A named client with no metric, which means the relationship existed and the outcome is unstated.
  3. 03A metric with no client, which cannot be corroborated by anyone.
  4. 04A rating with no profile behind it.
  5. 05A dashboard screenshot cropped just above the date range.
  6. 06A logo grid, which proves only that someone had access to the logo files.

Then the timeline, which is where most proof theatre falls apart. Search results move slowly. One large firm in the same market tells prospects publicly that SEO typically takes 3 to 6 months to show meaningful results, and saying that out loud is the honest move. We publish no speed promise at all. We also think any case study implying an instant outcome deserves suspicion, and that includes ours. If a result on this page ever looks too fast for the window attached to it, question the window first.

Questions Answered in the open

Three questions, answered without a click.

Are these results typical?

No. A case study is by definition the exception someone chose to publish. Read these for the shape of the work, not the size of the number, and treat any agency presenting its ceiling as its average with care.

Why do some panels say illustrative?

Because the figure behind them is not yet tied to a window and a method we can publish. Saying so costs us less than being caught, and it is worth more to you than a number you cannot test.

Can you guarantee this result for me?

No, and nobody honest would. One large competitor answers the guarantee question by stating that no agency can promise a particular keyword or a permanent position, then substitutes a commitment to sound practice. The first half of that is fair. The second half is not an answer. Our answer is a narrower promise rather than a louder one: a first-page placement, scoped in writing.
