Scope

What brand work covers.

Representative service copy, pending owner confirmation

There is no published methodology to draw from, so this section is written to be replaced by the real one rather than defended.

  • Identity marks. A primary mark plus the variants a real business actually needs: horizontal, stacked, icon-only and single-colour. Most identity problems in practice are not the logo, they are the four versions of it nobody made.
  • Palette. A defined set with the contrast checked for accessibility, not a mood board. A palette that fails on a phone in daylight is a palette that will be quietly abandoned by whoever builds the next page.
  • Type. A working type system, sans-serif per the owner's stated preference, with a display role and a body role defined, chosen for how it behaves at the sizes your pages genuinely use rather than at headline size on a presentation slide.
  • Tone. How the business sounds in a heading, in a service page and in a follow-up email, written down so it can be applied by somebody who was not in the room when it was decided.
  • Application. The identity carried across the site templates, the campaign assets, the ads and the profiles, so the same business appears everywhere the campaign puts it. This is the part that is normally lost in the handover, and it is the reason all of it sits inside one contract here.

How it begins

Discovery, then direction, then application.

Representative and swappable, pending the real terms

No process has ever been published by this business, so the three stages below are how the work is intended to run, written to be replaced rather than defended.

  1. Discovery. What the business actually is, and who it is competing against in the result list. That second half is the one most brand processes skip, and it is the one this arrangement is built around.
  2. Direction. A small number of routes, shown as real applications rather than as abstractions. A route presented on a blank background tells you almost nothing about how it will behave on a service page.
  3. Application. The rollout across the site and the campaign assets, and then the ongoing upkeep as new assets are produced each month.

Two properties matter more than the stages. The work is continuous rather than a project with a handover date, and it continues in a month billing is paused.

The mechanic

How this is assessed and billed.

  1. A monthly retainer is agreed up front.
  2. Each month is assessed against the ranking metrics named in the contract.
  3. In a month we are off target, the brand work continues and your billing stops.
  4. Billing resumes when results are back on track.

You stop paying for that month. The obligation is suspended rather than erased, and the work carries on through it.
