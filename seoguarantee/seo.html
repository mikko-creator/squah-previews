Clause 02The Pain

The report proves the work happened. It never says whether you rank.

Businesses reach us with the same three symptoms in the same order. New leads have dried up. The site gets little or no traffic. What traffic does arrive converts into nothing. We hear it from dental practices, medical spas, law firms, dispensaries, construction and home services firms, and real estate businesses, and the underlying sentence is identical every time: the customers are searching, and they are finding somebody else.

Then the SEO invoice arrives with a report attached.

  • Pages optimized this month.
  • Links built.
  • Meta descriptions rewritten.
  • Keywords tracked.
  • Content pieces delivered.

Every line is real work, every line is measurable, and not one line answers the only question you asked when you signed: are we on page one yet.

That is not an accident of reporting. When the thing being sold is activity, activity is what gets counted, and the ranking becomes a hope attached to the invoice rather than the thing the invoice is for. Nobody is accountable for the position, so the position is the one number the report leaves out.

Clause 04Scope

The actual scope of the SEO work

Technical remediation.

Crawl and indexation problems, site speed, redirects, duplicate and thin pages, internal link structure. A site that search engines cannot read cleanly will not rank whatever else is done to it, so this runs first.

Keyword and intent mapping.

The agreed terms get sorted by what the searcher actually wants, then mapped to the page that should answer it. One page per intent, rather than five pages competing with each other for the same phrase.

On-page optimization.

Titles, headings, page structure, internal linking and the copy on the pages that have to rank.

Content briefs and writing.

Pages and articles built against the agreed keyword set, so the writing plan and the ranking plan are one plan rather than two.

Link acquisition.

Earning references from sites that carry weight in your category, done as ongoing work rather than as a one-off purchase.

Local search, where you sell locally.

Google Business Profile, map visibility and the location pages that support them, handled as part of the engagement for businesses whose customers search by city.

The web and content work required to execute all of this is included rather than quoted. Six modules ship under one monthly plan: search engine optimization, website design and development, content writing, reputation management, conversion optimization, and graphic design. The guarantee attaches to the rankings. The other five exist because we cannot rank a site we are not allowed to fix.

Clause 06The Timeline

What this replaces: the open-ended retainer

The published expectation in this category is that SEO takes 3 to 6 months to show meaningful results, and that some businesses only see the more significant movement inside 12 months. A researched agency states that frame in its own copy, and as a description of how search works it is broadly correct. Ranking is slow, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something they do not control.

So the timeline is not what we take issue with. It is what the timeline gets used for. It converts the first half of a year into a stretch where nothing can be judged and nobody can be held to anything. You pay monthly, you read reports, and the answer to every question is that it is still early. At the end of it there is no commitment waiting, only a renewal date and the same horizon shifted forward.

We are not promising to be faster. We do not publish a ranking date, and we will not, because the index is not ours to schedule. What changes is what you are holding while the months pass. Instead of an open-ended retainer measured in activity, you have a stated first-page commitment against a named keyword set, with a window and a consequence attached to it, and a report that scores that set every month so you can see where you stand rather than being told it is early.

Clause 08Questions

  1. 01

    How long until I rank?

    Months, and we would rather say so plainly than sell you a date. The harder and more commercial the term, the longer it takes. We publish no ranking-speed promise and no timeframe of our own, because the only honest version of one would be a guess about a system we do not own. What we will do is tell you where each covered keyword stands every month, from the first report onward, so the wait is visible rather than described.

  2. 02

    Which keywords are covered?

    The ones agreed with you in writing at the outset, chosen for the terms your business actually sells on rather than the ones that are easy to win. The covered list is recorded, and the monthly report scores that list.

    Owner gap

    the eligibility rules for a qualifying keyword, and the number of terms each plan covers, are to be published here. Those are owner-supplied facts and none has been invented for this page.

  3. 03

    Can rankings really be guaranteed?

    The suspicion usually arrives as one of two questions. "If everything is included, what is the catch?" and this one. Both deserve a straight answer. On the second, one of the category's award-winning agencies states the standard position in its own copy: "No SEO agency can guarantee you will rank for a particular keyword or that you'll stay there forever." As written, that is true. Nobody controls Google's index, nobody controls what a competitor does next quarter, and nobody can promise a position in perpetuity. The usual response is to keep the word "guarantee" and quietly repoint it at process and effort, which every agency delivers anyway. We do the opposite. The word stays pointed at placement, and the promise gets narrow enough to survive the objection: named keywords, a stated window, a stated remedy, stated exclusions, published where you can read them before you sign. The credibility rests on us carrying the downside, not on any claim to control the algorithm.
