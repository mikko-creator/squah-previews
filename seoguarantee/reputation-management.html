02The failure

You can rank first and still lose the call

Businesses hire us for one job. They need to rank high on Google, because the leads have dried up, the site gets little or no traffic, and the traffic that does arrive converts into nothing. Dental practices, medical spas, law firms, dispensaries, construction and home services firms, real estate businesses. Different verticals, same sentence every time: the customers are searching, and they are finding somebody else.

Figure 02

Ranking solves half of that, and only half. A searcher who finds you does not read your title tag and dial. They look right, at the stars next to the listing, at how many reviews are behind them, at the date on the most recent one, at what the last unhappy customer wrote and whether anybody from the business ever answered. That comparison happens in seconds, on the results page, before your site has finished loading.

So the money you spent on the position gets spent again undoing what sits beside it. A profile with a handful of reviews reads as a business nobody uses. A profile whose newest review is years old reads as a business that closed. A complaint with no reply under it reads as a complaint that was true. None of that is a ranking problem, and none of it can be fixed by ranking harder. The call goes to the business two positions below you whose profile answers the question yours left open.

04Scope

What the module actually covers

This module is one of six services in every plan. The other five are search engine optimization, website design and development, content writing, conversion rate optimization, and graphic design and branding. Inside the reputation module there are four areas of work.

Profile monitoring.

Your business profiles across the platforms that carry weight in your category are watched for new reviews, rating movement and changes to the listing itself. What you get from it is timing. A reply written the same week reads very differently from one written six months later.

Review generation.

A workflow for asking your customers to review you, run on the cadence your business actually operates at, so the profile stays current instead of holding a rating that was earned two years ago. Volume matters here, but recency matters more, because a reader treats an old review as history and a recent one as fact.

Response templates and replies.

Prepared responses for the situations that recur in your category, positive and negative, so a reply goes up quickly and in your voice. An answered complaint is a different object to a reader than an unanswered one. It shows a business that is still paying attention.

Listing and NAP consistency.

NAP means name, address and phone number, and it means them as they appear everywhere your business is listed rather than only on your own site. Search engines read those entries as corroboration. When they disagree with each other, the corroboration weakens, and local visibility goes with it.

05The pattern

What a checkable review profile looks like

A rating is only proof when the reader can go and look at it. The pattern we build toward is small and strict.

  1. 01The score is a link, not an image of a score.
  2. 02The count beside it is the live count, not a number typed in once.
  3. 03The date of the most recent review is visible, so recency can be judged rather than assumed.
  4. 04Replies are visible under the reviews that needed one.
  5. 05Every proof element on this site either links to its source or is labelled as an illustration, so you never have to guess which one you are reading.

08Questions

01

Can you remove a bad review?

No. Nobody can delete a legitimate review from a platform they do not own, and we are not going to imply otherwise. What we can do is narrower and it is real. Content that breaks a platform's own policy, such as a fake review, a review left on the wrong business, or one that crosses into defamation, can be reported to that platform, which then decides. Everything else gets answered in public and outweighed over time by a steady flow of genuine current reviews. If an agency offers to remove or bury honest criticism, they are describing something that either breaks the platform rules or does not work.

02

How do you generate reviews?

By asking your customers, close to the moment the work is done, through a request workflow we run rather than hand you as homework. The asking goes to your customers generally, not only to the ones expected to be kind, because filtering the request is against the major platforms' rules and reads as false the moment anyone looks at the pattern. We do not offer incentives for reviews, for the same reason.

03

Which platforms?

Google Business Profile comes first, because that is the listing sitting directly beside the ranking you are paying for. Beyond it, the platforms worth the effort are the ones your buyers actually open, and those differ by category. A law firm and a dispensary are not judged in the same places.

04

What will you not claim on my behalf?

Anything we cannot show you the source of. No press or media endorsement without a working link to the article behind it. No rating, badge or certification presented as proof unless it links to the live source that issued it. No ranking-timeframe promise, because the index is not ours to schedule. No absolute phrasing, which means we do not promise a fixed position at the top of the results and we do not promise an overnight outcome. Generated examples are labelled as illustrative wherever they appear. The standard we hold your profile to is the one we hold this site to.
