Inside the Full Suite

Branding and graphic design, included

The product is called the First Page SEO Guarantee, and it is sold as one thing: the Full Suite. Web design, UI and UX, web development, SEO and conversion optimization run under a single monthly figure, and the only thing charged for is the guaranteed first-page ranking.

Branding and graphic design sit inside that suite. Not as an upgrade, not as a companion service with its own scope document, and not as a line item that appears on the invoice the first time a campaign needs artwork.

The second problem

Three vendors, three versions of the same company

Figure 02

The businesses that reach us are usually in the same position. Leads have slowed, the site gets little traffic, and the traffic that does arrive converts into nothing. It reads the same whether the business is a dental practice, a medical spa, a law firm, a dispensary, a construction or home services company or a real estate firm. Ranking is the reason they call.

What surfaces once we start work is a second problem nobody rang about. The site was built by one vendor, the social creative comes from a second, the trade-show graphics came from a third four years ago, and each of them re-derived the brand from whatever the previous one left behind. The purple is not quite the same purple. The headings are set in a face nobody can name. Two versions of the tagline are in circulation and neither is written down as the right one.

Then somebody asks for the logo in vector, and it turns out there is no vector. There is a PNG that was pulled off the website, and it was pulled off the website because that was the only place it existed. It is fine at the size it already is and soft at any other size, so every printer, every sponsorship pack and every partner listing gets a slightly worse copy of the last one. None of this is a crisis on any single day. It compounds quietly, and it makes a business that is buying first-page rankings look smaller than it is at the moment a searcher finally arrives.

What changes

Three things change when the design work is in the plan

Scope of the module

What the design work actually covers

Logo and mark work.

The primary mark, the variants a real business needs (horizontal, stacked, single color, reversed out of a dark ground, a small square mark for avatars and favicons) and clear-space and minimum-size rules so it survives being used by people who are not designers. One place holding the current files, in the formats the people who ask for them actually need. Print, screen, document templates, social profile assets. The point is that the next request is answered from the library instead of from someone's downloads folder.

Campaign and social creative.

Artwork for the campaigns the plan runs: paid social, display, organic posts, offer graphics, seasonal variants. Built from the same rails as the site, so a campaign asset and a landing page do not look like they came from two different businesses.

The photography, illustration and graphic treatment used across the pages themselves, handled to one consistent standard rather than sourced ad hoc per page. Consistent crops, consistent color handling, consistent weight against the type.

All of it is inside the same monthly plan as the other five modules: search engine optimization, website design and development, content writing, reputation management and conversion optimization. Six modules, one price. The guarantee attaches to the rankings, and the design work is included because the pages being ranked have to look like they belong to a business worth calling.

Before rebuilding this site we inventoried what our own identity actually consisted of, the same way we start a client engagement. The result, captured 2026-07-27: two logo files, both raster PNG. The primary is 4,632 bytes. The secondary is 2,957 bytes. There is no vector version of either one, anywhere.

That is the exact condition described further up this page, found in our own asset folder. It is also the reason the audit is the first thing we do rather than the thing we skip. You cannot fix a brand rail you have not written down, and most businesses have never been shown the list.

What it displaces

What this replaces: design bought one asset at a time

Figure 06

The arrangement this displaces is design commissioned per asset or per campaign from a separate studio. Every request starts the same sequence. A brief gets written because the studio does not carry your context. A quote gets issued because the work was not covered by anything already agreed. An approval gets chased, and a queue gets joined. By the time the artwork lands, the campaign it was drawn for has usually moved.

The cost that never appears on any of those quotes is drift. Each vendor rebuilds the brand from the last artifact they were sent, which is itself a rebuild, and a few rounds of that produce a company with several slightly different faces and no authority on which one is correct.

A written type system is what stops that. The rails we captured for this brand read: Montserrat carrying display and headings across a 300-900 weight range, with Roboto and Roboto Slab underneath for body text. Recorded that way, weights and pairings stop being a preference re-argued on every job, and when the modernization moves a face it moves once, in one document, and everything produced afterwards follows it. That record travels with the engagement. It is not held in the head of whichever designer took the last request.

Common questions

Questions we get asked about branding and graphic design

Do I get a new logo?

Sometimes, and it depends on what you already have. If your mark works and you hold usable files, we keep it and build the rest of the system around it. If the only version in existence is a small raster file that cannot be printed or scaled, redrawing it is inside the plan rather than a separate commission. Which of those applies to you is settled in the free consultation, by looking at your actual files.

Do I own the assets?

No terms have been supplied, so none have been written here. We are not going to describe an arrangement that has not been agreed.

Will it match my existing brand?

That is the default, not the exception. We sample your live rails before proposing anything, which is what we did here: the dominant purple came back as #621281, sitting alongside the neighboring #611180 and #771079, with magenta and gold as the accents and #010101 as the near-black ground the bright bands are laid over. Those values were measured off the existing stylesheet rather than picked fresh. Where we do propose a change, you get the reason and the before and after, and the decision stays yours.
