Clause 02The Objection

The invoice was certain. The outcome was not.

Most people reading this have bought SEO before. You paid a monthly fee, you received a monthly report, and you never found out whether the work moved anything or who was accountable if it did not.

So the first reaction to an offer like ours is suspicion, and it usually arrives as one question.

Here is the honest part. One of the category's award-winning agencies states the standard defence plainly, in its own copy:

As written, that is true. Nobody controls Google's index, nobody controls what a competitor does next quarter, and nobody can promise a position in perpetuity. We are not going to pretend otherwise, and we are not going to answer it with a louder claim. We answer it by making our promise narrow enough to survive that objection, and by publishing the boundary where you can read it before you sign.

Clause 06The Timeline

Search is slow.

This replaces the horizon you are asked to fund on trust. The published expectation in this category is that SEO takes 3 to 6 months to show meaningful results, and that some businesses only see the larger movement inside 12 months. A researched agency states that frame publicly, and as a description of how ranking works it is broadly right.

The timeline is not the problem. What it gets used for is. It turns the first half of a year into a period where nothing can be judged and nobody can be held to anything: you pay monthly, you read reports, and the answer to every question is that it is still early.

We are not promising to beat that timeline. Any agency that promises you a ranking date is selling you something it does not control. What changes here is what you hold while you wait. The fee buys a stated first-page commitment, with a stated window and a stated consequence, so the months are spent working towards something you can check at the end of them instead of towards another report.

Clause 08Questions

01

Can anyone really guarantee rankings?

Not for any keyword, forever. That objection is correct, and the usual response in this category is to keep the word and change what it points at. One award-winning agency defines its guarantee as a commitment to best practices, a deep understanding of search engine optimization, and dedication to pursuing the highest possible organic rankings. Read it closely and it guarantees effort, not placement. Every agency delivers effort. Our answer runs the other way: keep the word pointed at placement, then make it specific enough to be checkable, with agreed keywords, a stated window and a stated remedy. A narrow promise you can inspect is worth more than a broad one you cannot.
